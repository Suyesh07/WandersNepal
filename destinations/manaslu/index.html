Overview: The Untamed Himalayan Circuit

Circling the world's eighth-highest peak, Mount Manaslu (8,163m)—derived from the Sanskrit word Manasa, meaning "Intellect" or "Soul"—the Manaslu Circuit is celebrated as the pinnacle of wilderness tea-house trekking in Nepal. First opened to foreign trekkers under strict permit quotas in 1991, this remote border circuit in northern Gorkha district delivers the untamed isolation that the Annapurna Circuit possessed decades ago before road expansion.

The expedition traces the narrow granite chasm carved by the wild Budhi Gandaki river, crossing thrilling steel suspension bridges, cliff-carved cantilevers, and cascading waterfalls. Over two weeks of walking, you transition from Gurung and Magar terraced farmland through dense pine, rhododendron, and bamboo forests into the high, dry trans-Himalayan Tibetan realm of the Nubri Valley. Here, villages like Lho, Samagaun, and Samdo preserve centuries-old Tibetan Buddhist traditions, massive mani walls, and sacred gompas beneath the hanging icefalls of Manaslu. The route culminates in an arduous pre-dawn crossing over the wind-scoured Larkya La Pass (5,106m), descending directly into the Annapurna Conservation Area at Dharapani.

Why Choose the Manaslu Circuit?

Reason 01 The True Natural Circuit Experience

A complete geographic loop that crosses ecological zones from low subtropical river canyons (700m) to glaciated high-altitude passes (5,106m) without retracing steps.

Reason 02 Low Trekker Density & Wilderness Purity

Because the government enforces restricted-area quotas and mandatory guides, Manaslu sees a fraction of the crowds found in Khumbu or Annapurna, preserving authentic teahouse hospitality.

Reason 03 Ancient Nubri and Tsum Valley Heritage

Walk through historical Bhotia settlements where Tibetan dialects, traditional wooden architecture, yak husbandry, and sacred Nyingma monasteries have endured untouched for centuries.

Top Treks & Experiences on the Circuit

The Manaslu route offers rewarding side excursions and cultural waypoints along the main loop:

  • Cross the Glaciated Larkya La Pass (5,106m): A strenuous 8-to-10-hour push from Dharamsala over frozen moraines and prayer-flag crests, delivering views of Himlung Himal, Cheo Himal, Kang Guru, and Annapurna II.
  • Day Hike to Manaslu Base Camp (4,800m): An exceptional acclimatization hike from Samagaun up through birch woodlands and alpine moraines to the expedition tent city beneath the north face.
  • Visit Sacred Pungyen Gompa (4,050m): A high hidden plateau hike from Samagaun beneath the sheer southern ice wall of Manaslu, home to a 400-year-old sanctuary where monks retreat for solitary meditation.
  • Hike to Birendra Tal (Glacial Lake, 3,450m): Stroll along the milky turquoise waters of this glacial meltwater lake situated directly below the tongue of the Manaslu Glacier.
  • Explore Ribung Gompa in Lho Village: Climb to this vibrant hilltop Buddhist monastery overlooking traditional timber-roofed houses, framing an iconic sunrise view of Mount Manaslu’s double peak.
  • Trek to the Tibetan Border at Rui La (4,998m): A memorable day excursion from Samdo following the ancient salt trade caravan route to the high mountain pass bordering the Tibetan Autonomous Region.
  • Optional Detour into the Sacred Tsum Valley: Add 5 to 7 days to explore the "Valley of Happiness" (Beyul Kyimolung), visiting Milarepa’s Cave and the ancient nunneries of Mu Gompa and Rachen Gompa.

Key Villages, Waypoints & Acclimatization Hubs

  • Machha Khola (869m): The standard roadhead terminus where rough jeep tracks from Arughat end and traditional foot trails begin along the Budhi Gandaki.
  • Jagat (1,340m): A paved flagstone village serving as the primary government checkpoint where Restricted Area Permits (RAP) and MCAP cards are verified.
  • Deng (1,860m): The geographic boundary where lower Hindu and Gurung villages transition into the Tibetan-influenced culture of the upper valley.
  • Namrung (2,660m): The gateway to the Nubri Valley, featuring apple orchards, paved stone streets, and comfortable modern stone teahouses.
  • Lho (3,180m): A picturesque village framed beneath the summit of Manaslu, celebrated for Ribung Gompa and vast fields of high-altitude barley.
  • Samagaun (3,530m): The largest village in upper Nubri and the primary two-night acclimatization base, filled with mani walls, yak pastures, and historic monasteries.
  • Samdo (3,860m): The final permanent village before the pass, settled by Tibetan refugees in the 1950s, located at the crossroads of the ancient trade trail into Tibet.
  • Dharamsala / Larkya Phedi (4,460m): A high alpine stone shelter base camp set below the moraine, providing an overnight stop before the pass crossing.
  • Bimthang (3,590m): A breathtaking wide meadow basin reached on the descent from Larkya La, surrounded by pine forests, glaciers, and mountain peaks.

How to Get There: Roadheads & Transport Logistics

The Manaslu Circuit is accessed entirely overland from Kathmandu, avoiding domestic mountain flights:

  • Kathmandu to Trailhead (Machha Khola): Take an early morning (6:00 AM) public bus or private 4WD Scorpio jeep from Kathmandu via the Prithvi Highway to Dhading Besi, continuing over unpaved mountain roads past Arughat and Soti Khola to Machha Khola (approx. 140 km, 8–10 hours drive). A private 4WD jeep costs approx. NPR 20,000–25,000 one-way and is strongly advised over crowded local buses.
  • The Circuit Exit (Dharapani to Besisahar): The trek concludes at Dharapani (1,860m), where the Manaslu trail joins the Annapurna Circuit road. From Dharapani, shared mountain jeeps run along the Marsyangdi gorge road to Besisahar (approx. 3–4 hours, NPR 2,000–2,500 per seat).
  • Besisahar to Kathmandu or Pokhara: From Besisahar, frequent microbuses, tourist coaches, and private taxis connect back to Kathmandu (5–6 hours) or Pokhara (3–4 hours).
Monsoon Roadhead Conditions: The dirt road between Arughat, Soti Khola, and Machha Khola is vulnerable to monsoon landslides (July–August). During early autumn (September), jeeps may occasionally terminate at Soti Khola, requiring an extra day of walking along the river track.

Permits & Regulations (2026 Updated Rates)

Because Manaslu borders Tibet, the Department of Immigration strictly regulates access through a three-permit system plus a local municipal fee:

  • Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP):
    • Peak Autumn Season (September to November): USD 100 per person for the first 7 days, plus USD 15 per day for each additional day inside the restricted zone (Jagat to Dharapani).
    • Off-Peak Season (December to August): USD 75 per person for the first 7 days, plus USD 10 per day thereafter.
  • Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP): NPR 3,000 (approx. USD 23) for foreign nationals; NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals.
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP): NPR 3,000 (approx. USD 23) for foreign nationals; NPR 1,000 for SAARC. (Required because the circuit terminates and exits through Dharapani in the Annapurna conservation zone).
  • Chumnubri Rural Municipality Fee: NPR 1,000–2,000 (approx. USD 10), collected locally at the Jagat checkpoint for village trail maintenance.
  • Mandatory Guide Regulation: Foreign trekkers are legally required to hike with an accredited, government-licensed Nepali guide arranged through an authorized agency. Permits must be processed through an agency. Solo trekkers are permitted provided they are accompanied by their licensed guide.

How Long to Stay: Route Duration Models

12–13 Days Fast-Paced Circuit

For fit hikers with previous high-altitude experience: rapid approach from Machha Khola, one acclimatization day in Samagaun, Larkya La crossing, and jeep exit from Dharapani.

14–16 Days The Classic Manaslu Circuit

The recommended baseline: comfortable daily pacing, two full acclimatization nights in Samagaun, side trips to Birendra Tal and Manaslu Base Camp, and unhurried descent via Bimthang.

20–22 Days Manaslu Circuit & Tsum Valley Grand Loop

The comprehensive expedition: branches into the sacred Tsum Valley to visit Mu Gompa and Milarepa's Cave before rejoining the main circuit at Deng to cross Larkya La.

Best Time to Trek: Weather, Passes & Visibility

Season Months Atmospheric Conditions & Larkya La Status
Autumn (Peak Season) Oct – Nov Optimal Conditions: Post-monsoon atmospheric clarity, dry trails, deep blue skies, and stable high-pressure windows over Larkya La. Valley days are warm (15–20°C); nights at Dharamsala drop to -8°C to -12°C. Teahouses operate at peak capacity.
Spring (Flowering & Warming) Mar – May Warmer hiking days, longer daylight hours, and hillsides ablaze with blooming red and pink rhododendrons between Deng and Namrung. Snow over Larkya La melts steadily; occasional afternoon clouds build over high ridges.
Winter (Severe Snow & Cold) Dec – Feb Dry and sunny, but extreme cold settles across upper Nubri. Temperatures at Dharamsala plunge below -18°C. Larkya La can become blocked by deep snow drifts, and teahouses above Samdo often close as residents migrate south.
Monsoon (Heavy Rain & Leeches) Jun – Sep Torrential rainfall in the lower Budhi Gandaki gorge, high landslide risk, slippery trails, and active leeches. The river swells significantly, and Larkya La is shrouded in cloud cover. Trekking is not recommended.

Top Pick: Mid-October to mid-November delivers the highest probability of clear weather for crossing Larkya La; April offers longer daylight hours and blooming rhododendron forests.

Where to Stay: Teahouse Infrastructure on the Circuit

Over the past decade, Manaslu has evolved from a mandatory tent-camping expedition into a fully functional teahouse trekking route:

  • Lower River Canyon (Machha Khola to Jagat): Simple family-run lodges built with stone masonry. Rooms feature twin wooden beds with foam mattresses and shared indoor squat or western toilets. Hot solar showers are widely available.
  • Nubri Valley Villages (Namrung, Lho, Samagaun): Surprisingly comfortable modern stone lodges. Several properties in Namrung and Lho feature attached bathrooms, heated dining rooms, bakeries with fresh coffee, and solar lighting.
  • High Mountain Outposts (Samdo, Dharamsala, Bimthang): Infrastructure at Dharamsala (Larkya Phedi, 4,460m) is basic—consisting of stone huts and seasonal prefabricated rooms. Rooms are unheated and sub-zero at night. A four-season sleeping bag (rated -15°C) is essential. Bimthang, on the other side of the pass, offers cozy wooden lodges set in a scenic mountain amphitheater.

Trail Food & Safe Water Protocols

Dining menus along the Manaslu Circuit are freshly prepared, hearty, and geared toward sustaining energy on long mountain ascents:

  • Dal Bhat Power: Steamed white rice, yellow lentil soup, spiced potato curry, and mustard greens (saag) with unlimited refills—the freshest, most fuel-efficient choice in every teahouse.
  • High-Altitude Specialties: Sherpa vegetable noodle stew (Syakpa), Tibetan flatbreads, potato rosti with yak cheese, tsampa porridge (roasted barley flour), vegetable momos, and garlic soup (widely consumed to aid breathing).
  • Local Produce: Sample fresh apples and organic dried apple chips in Namrung and fresh yak curd in Samagaun. Avoid meat past Namrung, as animal slaughter is prohibited in Buddhist Nubri and meat is portaged up without refrigeration.
  • Safe Water Practice: Never drink unboiled tap or stream water. Carry two 1-liter reusable wide-mouth bottles (Nalgene) and treat all water using chlorine dioxide tablets or UV filtration devices (SteriPEN). Boiled drinking water can be purchased at teahouses every evening. Single-use plastic bottles are discouraged throughout the conservation area.

Indigenous Heritage: Nubri Traditions & Monastery Etiquette

The upper reaches of Manaslu are divided into two distinct cultural regions: Tsum (the valley of peace) and Nubri ("the western mountains"). Inhabited by Bhotia communities who migrated from Tibet centuries ago, Nubri maintains an unadulterated Tibetan Buddhist culture rooted in the Nyingma tradition.

Trail Etiquette & Respectful Conduct:

  • Clockwise Circumambulation: Always walk to the left of mani walls, stupas, and chortens, keeping the sacred stone monument on your right-hand side.
  • Monastery Decorum: Remove your shoes and hat before entering prayer halls in Ribung Gompa, Pungyen Gompa, or Samagaun. Do not touch sacred thangkas or murals, and ask permission before photographing monks or ceremonial prayer assemblies.
  • Respect the Non-Violence Zone (Ahimsa): The entire upper valley is a sacred sanctuary where hunting, animal slaughter, and trapping are strictly forbidden by community law. Treat working yaks, mules, and wildlife with gentle respect.

Altitude Sickness (AMS), Larkya La Hazards & Evacuation

The ascent of Larkya La Pass (5,106m) requires respect for mountain weather and acclimatization pacing:

  • Mandatory Acclimatization Pacing: Do not rush past Samagaun (3,530m). Take a dedicated active rest day in Samagaun, hiking higher to Manaslu Base Camp (4,800m) or Pungyen Gompa (4,050m) during the day and returning to sleep. Sleep at Samdo (3,860m) and Dharamsala (4,460m) before attempting the pass.
  • Crossing Larkya La Pass: Trekking teams depart Dharamsala before 4:30 AM to cross the pass before midday winds and cloud buildup. In early spring or late autumn, the moraine descent into Bimthang can be hard-packed ice and scree—carrying lightweight microspikes and trekking poles is mandatory.
  • Recognizing AMS: Throbbing headache, nausea, loss of appetite, and dizziness. Never ascend if you feel symptomatic. If symptoms worsen, descent is the only medical cure.
  • Emergency Helicopter Evacuation: Ensure your travel insurance policy explicitly covers emergency helicopter rescue up to 6,000 meters without altitude caps. In an emergency, your licensed guide coordinates with dispatch teams in Kathmandu.

Responsible Conservation in Manaslu

Preserving this fragile Himalayan corridor requires conscious trekker choices:

  • Pack out all personal non-biodegradable trash (snack wrappers, batteries, medicine blister packs) back down to Besisahar or Kathmandu for disposal.
  • Ensure your trekking agency enforces ethical porter load limits (maximum 20kg to 25kg) and provides comprehensive cold-weather clothing, sturdy footwear, and insurance for all support staff.
  • Stay in locally owned family teahouses and support village bakeries to ensure tourism revenue directly benefits the local Nubri economy.

Frequently Asked Questions About Manaslu

How difficult is the Manaslu Circuit compared to Annapurna or EBC?

The Manaslu Circuit is more strenuous than Everest Base Camp or the classic Annapurna Circuit. The route is 14 to 18 days long, the trail climbs through narrow, steep river canyons with significant vertical gains, teahouse amenities are more rustic, and the crossing of Larkya La Pass (5,106m) requires a demanding 8- to 10-hour day over icy moraines.

Can I trek the Manaslu Circuit independently without a guide in 2026?

No. By government law, the Manaslu Conservation Area is a restricted zone. Independent solo trekking is strictly illegal. Foreign trekkers must be accompanied by an accredited, government-licensed Nepali mountain guide arranged through an authorized agency. Solo travelers are permitted provided they hire a licensed guide.

Do I need crampons or technical climbing gear for Larkya La?

Technical climbing ropes, ice axes, or mountaineering boots are not required on the standard trail. However, carrying a pair of lightweight microspikes (slip-on trail crampons) and sturdy trekking poles is strongly recommended, especially in spring (March–April) or late autumn (November) when the moraine descent into Bimthang can be covered in hard-packed ice and snow.

What permits do I need for the Manaslu Circuit?

You need three main permits plus a local municipal fee: the Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP, USD 75–100/week), the Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP, NPR 3,000), the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP, NPR 3,000 for the Dharapani exit), and the Chumnubri Rural Municipality entry fee (NPR 1,000–2,000). Standard TIMS cards are not required for Manaslu.

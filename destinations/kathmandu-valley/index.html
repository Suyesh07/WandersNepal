Kathmandu Valley Overview

Kathmandu hits you the moment you step outside your hotel. The scent of incense, temple bells and motorbikes fills the narrow brick streets. Many travelers treat the city as a brief stop before an Everest Base Camp or Annapurna Circuit trek, but spending two or three days here reveals some of the best things to do in Kathmandu.

The valley's historic urban centers include Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur. Their palace squares, Buddhist stupas and Hindu temple precincts reflect centuries of Newar craftsmanship and religious tradition.

This guide covers practical visitor tips, heritage sites and local neighborhoods. For a ready-made plan, see our 3-day Kathmandu itinerary.

Best Places to Visit in Kathmandu

The Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage property comprises seven monument zones: the Durbar Squares of Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur, plus four religious ensembles. Start with these four widely visited sites to experience the valley's Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

Kathmandu Durbar Square | Basantapur Durbar Square

Kathmandu Durbar Square, also known as Basantapur Durbar Square, is in the old town's center. This was the site where old kings lived and ruled Nepal. People gather here every afternoon. The square is full of red-brick temples, open yards, and hand-carved wooden windows.

The Kumari Ghar is a red-brick residence where the Kumari, a young girl revered by local Newar communities, may appear at a third-floor window. Respect posted photography restrictions and do not photograph her.

Kaal Bhairav, a giant stone statue of Lord Shiva, stands across the open brick ground, with a dark face and wide eyes. Accused criminals were brought here to tell the truth, as people believed lying in front of the statue would bring bad luck. Right nearby is Kasthamandap, a large wooden pavilion built from the timber of a single Sal tree, which gave the city of Kathmandu its name.

For foreigners, tickets are NPR 1,000, and for SAARC nationals, they're NPR 150 to NPR 500, according to the Nepal Tourism Board Heritage Site Entry Fees page.

Boudhanath Stupa

Boudhanath Stupa is one of the world's largest stupas and a center of Tibetan Buddhist life in Nepal. Its white dome and golden tower, painted with the Buddha's eyes, rise above the surrounding neighborhood.

The best thing you can do here is join the morning walk, known as a kora. At around 6:30 AM, monks in deep red robes, older pilgrims, and local residents start walking clockwise around the dome. Burning juniper leaves fill the air, brass prayer wheels spin, and people chant quiet Buddhist prayers.

After you finish walking around the base, climb up the stairs to a rooftop cafe around the square. Watching the crowd circle below while drinking a warm cup of milk tea or Tibetan butter tea is one of the most relaxing things to do in Kathmandu.

Foreign travelers pay NPR 400 to enter, while SAARC nationals pay NPR 100. Remember to walk clockwise around the dome and never sit on the prayer wheel railings.

Swayambhunath Monkey Temple

Sitting high on a green hill on the west side of town, Swayambhunath is one of the most famous places to see in Kathmandu. The temple is home to hundreds of monkeys that live in the trees and roam across the terraces, earning it the nickname Monkey Temple.

Getting to the top gives you good exercise. The climb involves walking up 365 stone steps under shady trees, with monkeys playing on the railings beside you. At the top, you'll see the white dome, colorful prayer flags, and golden tower rising into the sky.

From the top terrace, you can see the entire Kathmandu Valley. After a rain, on clear days, you can see green hills and snowy mountains in the distance, beyond the city rooftops.

Tickets cost NPR 200 for foreign tourists and NPR 50 for SAARC visitors. These monkeys are fast and curious, so keep your snacks, fruit, and open water bottles secure in your bag to prevent them from grabbing them.

Pashupatinath Temple

Pashupatinath, beside the sacred Bagmati River, is Nepal's holiest Hindu temple and is dedicated to Lord Shiva.

The inner golden temple is restricted to Hindu worshippers, but the riverbanks are open to everyone. Visitors can stroll along the river, see small shrines, and explore the gardens. Hindu funeral fires are held daily on the stone steps by the river. Watching from across the river, visitors get a glimpse of Hindu beliefs on life and death.

People gather on the stone steps across from the main temple at 6:00 PM every evening. The holy fire ceremony, called the Aarti, is a big draw. Hindu priests use heavy brass lamps filled with burning oil, and they ring bells and blow conch shells; crowds sing along. It's very memorable to watch this ceremony by the river in the warm evening firelight; it's one of the top spiritual things to do in Kathmandu.

Tickets for the outer grounds are NPR 1,000 for foreign visitors, but to enter you must also dress modestly, covering your shoulders and knees. It's also important that you don't take pictures of families at the cremation platforms, and Indian citizens can visit for free.

Kathmandu Sightseeing Tour

Central Kathmandu isn't everything. You gotta cross the river to see Patan and Bhaktapur, two old kingdoms with a lot of charm. They're pretty and have many famous spots, like great tourist attractions.

Patan Durbar Square & Artisan Courtyards

Patan is south of Kathmandu, across the river, and was once called Lalitpur, or City of Beauty. Local artists in Patan work with wood, stone, and metal.

Patan Durbar Square is the center of town, with red-brick floors and stone temples. Inside the old palace is the Patan Museum, one of the best museums in Asia. The museum displays old bronze statues and wood carvings of the Buddha and Hindu gods.

Walk away from the main square and into the quiet bahal’s. The Golden Temple is a five-minute walk away, and it's an old Buddhist monastery with young priests. In the side lanes, you'll hear small hammers tapping metal as craftsmen make bronze statues.

Tickets cost NPR 1,000 for foreigners, including the Patan Museum, and NPR 250 for SAARC nationals. A taxi ride from Thamel takes about twenty minutes.

Bhaktapur Medieval City & Pottery Square

Bhaktapur is about 13 kilometers east of Kathmandu. The main heritage streets are car-free, so you can walk past old houses, temples and squares.

The five-story Nyatapola Temple, built in 1702, is in Taumadhi Square. It's the tallest temple in Nepal, guarded by large stone statues of wrestlers, elephants, and lions on its stairs. Pottery Square is nearby, where potters spin clay into cups and bowls.

Try a bowl of Juju Dhau, or the King of Curd, while walking in Bhaktapur. This thick, sweet yogurt is made from fresh buffalo milk and served in small red clay bowls.

Tickets to Bhaktapur cost NPR 1,800 for foreigners and NPR 500 for SAARC nationals. Spend a night in a small hotel if you have time, and walk the quiet alleys after the day visitors leave.

Changu Narayan Temple Ridge

Changu Narayan is on a green hill north of Bhaktapur. It's the oldest working temple in the Kathmandu Valley, built in the 5th century.

In the courtyard, you can see a stone pillar made in 464 AD by King Manadeva. It has the oldest carved writing found in Nepal. That temple is covered in old wood carvings of Lord Vishnu.

Changu Narayan is peaceful because it's far from the city. The path to the gate has village homes, pine trees, and family tea shops. Many people walk down to this temple from Nagarkot through pretty farmland, which you can read about in the Wanders Nepal Nagarkot Guide.

Entry tickets cost NPR 300 for foreign visitors and NPR 100 for SAARC visitors. You can get there by taxi from Bhaktapur or by walking from Nagarkot in the morning.

Things to Do in Thamel & Neighborhoods

Thamel is the main neighborhood where travelers stay, sleep, eat and buy gear. It's packed with clothes shops, bakeries, cafes and music bars, making it the center for fun things to do in Thamel and the heart of Kathmandu nightlife.

Shopping and Bookstores in Thamel

You can easily find clothes, gifts, and trekking gear in Thamel. For mountain treks, many shops offer warm jackets, walking poles, sleeping bags, and packs. You can find affordable local gear as well as original brand stores.

For gifts, you can buy warm yak-wool blankets, soft scarves, brass singing bowls, prayer flags, and paper notebooks made from local tree bark. Thamel also has big bookstores, like Pilgrims Book House, where you can look through trail maps, mountain books, and stories about Nepal.

In small gift shops, polite bargaining is the norm. You can offer 20% to 30% less than the initial price, but be sure to smile and stay friendly. At fixed-price bookshops and large gear shops, prices are non-negotiable.

Evening Dining and Live Music Venues

Thamel is the place to be in Kathmandu at night. The area features great live music, with local bands playing acoustic guitar and classic rock at popular venues like Purple Haze Rock Bar.

If you want a quiet place away from street noise, walk into the Garden of Dreams on the edge of Thamel. The garden boasts big green lawns, white pillars, lotus ponds, and calm seats, providing a peaceful oasis from the city.

For dinner, you can find all kinds of food. You can order a full plate of Dal Bhat with warm rice and lentil soup, eat freshly baked pizza, or enjoy hot momos and Tibetan noodle soup.

Exploring Spice and Brass Markets at Asan and Indra Chowk

Take a short walk from southern Thamel towards Durbar Square to experience local shopping. The walk takes you through the bustling market squares of Asan and Indra Chowk.

Asan Chowk is a vibrant market located at the intersection of six roads. Farmers sell fresh greens on cloth mats, while small shops display tall, neat piles of spices, including yellow turmeric, cumin, dried ginger, and red chilies. The front of the Annapurna Temple, above the square, is adorned with fresh marigold flowers.

A short walk further leads to Indra Chowk, where shops showcase handmade brass pots, copper water jugs, and vibrant glass bangles. This walk is only about 1.5 kilometers long and shows you real city life. Keep your phone and money in your front pockets because the small streets get crowded.

Fun Activities & Experiences with Friends

Kathmandu has plenty to offer if you're traveling with friends and want to try fun activities beyond temple visits. These are great places to visit in Kathmandu with friends and include some of the most memorable fun things to do in Kathmandu.

Newari Food Tours and Traditional Feasts

Learning about local culture is also pretty tasty when you're eating Newari food. Sitting down with friends for a traditional Samay Baji meal is a great experience; it's served on dried leaf plates.

This meal has many different tastes. You get beaten rice flakes, called baji, spicy grilled buffalo meat, or chhoyela, and soft fried lentil patties, called wo or bara. Chatamari is another favorite; it's a thin, crispy rice-flour bread that can have egg, vegetables, or minced meat on top.

During feasts, adults often drink aila, a local drink that's poured into small clay cups from up high. You can read more about these dishes in the Wanders Nepal Food & Culture Guide.

Day Hikes Around the Valley Rim (Nagarkot & Shivapuri)

City dust getting you down; going up to the green hills around the valley is an easy way to spend a day in fresh air.

One popular trail is Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park, which is on the north side of the valley. You walk up stone stairs, through cool oak trees, to Nagi Gompa, a peaceful monastery where Buddhist nuns live and study. That trail has clean air, and the views down over the valley are nice.

The ridge trail from Nagarkot to Changu Narayan is another easy walk. From Nagarkot, you can watch the sunrise over the high Himalayas, then walk down through pine trees, terraced farms, and small villages. Since these hills aren't very high, the walk is safe, easy, and comfortable for everyone.

Cooking Classes and Pottery Workshops

Learning a local craft is a fun way to spend an afternoon. In Thamel or Patan, you can join a half-day momo cooking class. A local cook will take you to buy vegetables, show you how to fold the dough, and teach you to make spicy tomato chutney. And the best part is, you get to eat your own plate of hot momos for lunch.

In Bhaktapur, Pottery Square is a place you can visit to sit at a wooden wheel. A local potter will help you shape wet clay into small cups or oil lamps, which you can keep as souvenirs.

Tired from flying or walking; you can try a singing bowl sound bath in Thamel or Boudhanath. A teacher rings heavy brass bowls around you, making deep humming sounds that help your body relax.

Practical Planning FAQ for Kathmandu

How many days do you need in Kathmandu?

Three to four full days is what you need to see the main temples, palace squares, and markets. No rush. With three days, you can visit Kathmandu Durbar Square, Swayambhunath, Boudhanath, Pashupatinath, and Patan, it's all in the Wanders Nepal 3-Day Itinerary. Five days and you can add a slow day in Bhaktapur, take it easy with a ridge hike, that's the Wanders Nepal 5-Day Plan. Seven days, you can mix it up, Kathmandu and side trips to Pokhara and Chitwan, on the Wanders Nepal 7-Day Route.

What are the entry fees for Kathmandu heritage sites?

Entry fees vary by site and visitor category. The Nepal Tourism Board currently lists Bhaktapur Durbar Square at NPR 1,800 for foreign nationals and NPR 500 for SAARC nationals; Patan Durbar Square with its museum and Kathmandu Durbar Square with the Tribhuvan Museum at NPR 1,000 for foreign nationals and NPR 500 for BIMSTEC nationals; Pashupatinath Temple at NPR 1,000 for foreign and SAARC nationals, with free entry for Indian nationals; Boudhanath Stupa at NPR 400 for foreign nationals and NPR 100 for SAARC nationals; Swayambhu Stupa at NPR 200 and NPR 50, respectively; and Changu Narayan at NPR 300 and NPR 100, respectively. Fees can change, so check the current Nepal Tourism Board fee table before visiting.

What is the best time of year to visit Kathmandu?

Autumn (October-November) is the ideal time to visit Kathmandu as the previous summers' rains will have washed away the dust and provided the city with beautiful clear blue skies, clean air and crisp, clear mountain views. In addition, autumn is the festive period; the main festivals of Dashain and Tihar take place during this time.

The spring season (March-April), offers another excellent window of opportunity when Kathmandu's temperature ranges from 20-26 degrees Celsius, providing perfect walking conditions. Additionally, the springtime brings new growth to the many trees in Kathmandu and allows visitors to experience the vibrant colors of Holi.

In winter (December-February), while there may be some sun in the afternoons, mornings tend to be chilly and nights often drop to 3 degrees Celsius, resulting in fewer tourists. However, the cooler months do provide better opportunities to view the surrounding mountains and valleys.

During the monsoon season (June-September), Kathmandu experiences high temperatures and heavy rainfall which creates mud on all hill roads making it difficult to travel by car, however the lower regions of the valley remain lush and green.

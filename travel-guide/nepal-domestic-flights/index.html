Navigating Nepal’s Skies

Domestic flights are the vital link connecting Kathmandu to remote mountain hubs like Lukla, Jomsom, and Simikot. Because Nepal’s topography features some of the highest terrain on earth, regional aviation operates under unique logistical constraints that every traveler must anticipate.

Mountain Flights Are Weather-Led

Unlike commercial jetliners operating on instrument flight rules (IFR), short takeoff and landing (STOL) aircraft flying into mountain airstrips depend strictly on Visual Flight Rules (VFR).

  • Cloud & Wind Disruptions: Morning fog, high crosswinds, or sudden cloud cover can delay or ground all flights for hours—or entire days—with very little advance notice.
  • The Backlog Effect: When flights are grounded for 24 hours, a backlog builds up. Priority is given chronologically, meaning flexibility in your overall itinerary is essential.

Airport Shifts & Ramechhap Rerouting

During peak trekking seasons (spring and autumn), air traffic congestion at Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) in Kathmandu forces the Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN) to shift all tourist flights heading to Lukla over to Manthali Airport in Ramechhap.

What This Means for You: If your flight is rerouted to Ramechhap, you must arrange a 4-to-5-hour midnight overland car or jeep transfer from Kathmandu to Manthali to catch early morning mountain departures. Always verify your departure airport with your agency ahead of time.

Baggage & Weight Restrictions

Due to the compact size of Twin Otter and Let L-410 aircraft serving mountain routes, strict weight limits apply:

  • Standard Allowance: Typically 10 kg of checked luggage plus 5 kg of hand luggage per passenger (total 15 kg).
  • Excess Luggage: Excess weight incurs heavy per-kilogram fees or may need to be left behind at your Kathmandu hotel storage. Pack light and prioritize modular layering systems.

Connection Buffers Protect Your Trip

Never book an international departure flight home on the same day you fly out of a weather-sensitive mountain airport like Lukla. Always protect your itinerary with a minimum 48-hour buffer in Kathmandu to absorb unexpected weather delays.

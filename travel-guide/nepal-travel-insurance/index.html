Why Standard Policies Fail in Nepal

Standard international travel insurance policies often assume flat-land city tours or low-elevation hiking. In Nepal, where popular trekking routes cross high mountain passes well above 4,000 or 5,000 meters, verifying your policy's fine print is essential before setting foot on the trail.

Read Altitude Limits Carefully

A policy that advertises "hiking coverage" may still contain a strict elevation ceiling (such as 3,000 meters).

If you plan to trek to Everest Base Camp (5,364m), Annapurna Circuit (Thorong La Pass at 5,416m), or Langtang, your policy must explicitly state coverage up to or exceeding **5,000 to 6,000 meters**. Double-check the fine print to ensure your maximum planned elevation matches your actual route.

Check Evacuation Wording

In remote Himalayan regions without road access, medical evacuation for severe altitude sickness or injury involves helicopter rescue.

  • Direct Payment vs Reimbursement: Clarify whether your insurer coordinates directly with rescue agencies (guaranteeing payment) or requires you to pay upfront out-of-pocket and seek reimbursement later.
  • Medical Approval: Confirm whether emergency helicopter dispatch requires prior authorization from the insurer’s 24/7 hotline or if local guides/doctors can initiate rescue in critical scenarios.

Declare All Planned Activities

Insurers categorize activities strictly. Standard policies may exclude adventure sports unless specifically added. Ensure your policy covers:

  • High-altitude teahouse trekking.
  • Optional activities such as Pokhara paragliding, whitewater rafting, or motorbike touring along hill highways.

Insurance Planning Checklist

  • Verify that your policy covers the exact maximum elevation of your trek.
  • Carry a printed copy of your policy number, emergency assistance numbers, and claim procedures.
  • Save digital copies offline on your smartphone.
  • Confirm whether weather-related domestic flight delays are covered under trip interruption clauses.

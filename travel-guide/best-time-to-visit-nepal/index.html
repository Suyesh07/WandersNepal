Choosing Your Weather Window

Nepal’s climate varies dramatically across geographic tiers—from the subtropical Terai plains to the arid high plateaus of Mustang and the glaciated ridges of the high Himalayas. Matching your travel goals to the right season ensures clear mountain views and safe trail conditions.

Autumn: October & November (Peak Season)

Following the summer monsoon rains, autumn brings the most stable weather of the year. Skies are typically crystal clear, mountain visibility is at its peak, and daytime temperatures are comfortable for walking.

  • Trail Conditions: Dry, firm walking paths across all major routes (Everest Base Camp, Annapurna Circuit, Manaslu).
  • Cultural Highlights: Nepal's biggest festivals—Dashain and Tihar—take place during this period, filling towns and villages with celebration and family gatherings.
  • Logistical Note: Teahouses and flights experience extremely high demand. Advance booking for domestic flights (Lukla/Pokhara) and teahouse rooms is essential.

Winter & Spring: December to May

Winter (December – February)

Mountain passes above 4,000 meters experience sub-zero temperatures and occasional snow blockages (such as Thorong La or Larkya La). However, winter is an ideal season for low-altitude cultural touring in the Kathmandu Valley, Pokhara, and jungle safaris in Chitwan, offering crisp air and fewer crowds.

Spring (March – May)

As winter recedes, temperatures rise steadily. Spring is famous for blooming rhododendron forests across the mid-hills (especially along Ghorepani Poon Hill and Langtang). Days are long, and although afternoon haze can occasionally soften distant mountain views, conditions remain fantastic for high-altitude trekking.

Monsoon & Rain-Shadow Regions (June – September)

The summer monsoon brings heavy rainfall across most of Nepal, leading to slick mountain trails, leeches in lower forests, afternoon cloud cover obstructing peaks, and occasional highway or domestic flight delays due to visibility limits.

The Rain-Shadow Exception: Regions lying north of the main Himalayan divide—such as Upper and Lower Mustang and parts of Dolpo—fall within a rain shadow blocked from monsoon clouds by Dhaulagiri and Annapurna. These areas remain dry, windy, and fully viable for trekking throughout June, July, and August.

Seasonal Planning Checklist

  • Check official weather advisories and domestic flight operational status close to departure.
  • Save offline maps and regional guide data to your device before heading into remote valleys.
  • Confirm exact teahouse inclusions, transport pickup points, and cancellation policies with your operator.
  • Always maintain a 48-hour weather buffer before international departure flights.

Route overview

A restricted high-desert journey built around permits, village stays and transport contingency.

Upper Mustang is a restricted-area journey, so permit conditions, registered support and the approved itinerary shape the route. The best plan makes room for village life rather than rushing between photogenic landmarks.

Transportation

Flight or road to Jomsom; jeep and/or trekking. Compare the full transfer—boarding point, luggage, stops and final arrival—not only the advertised travel time.

Last verified September 2026. Schedules and road conditions change; confirm directly before travel.

Major stops

  • Pokhara — Complete restricted-area logistics before departure; independent travel is not the planning assumption.
  • Jomsom — A transport hub where strong afternoon winds and flight disruption shape the schedule.
  • Kagbeni — The gateway to Upper Mustang and an important village in its own right.
  • Tsarang — A historic settlement of monastery, palace and cultivated fields beneath eroded cliffs.
  • Lo Manthang — Plan more than a rushed overnight to understand the walled town and surrounding valleys.
  • Jomsom return — Protect a weather day before connecting back to Pokhara or Kathmandu.

Where to stay & eat

Use locally run lodges where the permitted route allows and ask your registered operator how accommodation is selected. Food supplies are shaped by distance and season; eat simply, carry treated water and avoid creating unnecessary packaging waste.

Route alternatives

  • Use a jeep-supported itinerary to reduce walking days while retaining village stops.
  • Walk selected stages for more landscape context instead of driving the entire route.
  • Limit the trip to Lower Mustang if permit, time or weather constraints make Upper Mustang unrealistic.

Safety & useful tips

  • Travel only under the current restricted-area rules and approved arrangements.
  • Expect wind, dust, cold nights and limited medical access.
  • Protect a Jomsom weather buffer before onward flights.
  • Respect monasteries, photography restrictions and community water sources.

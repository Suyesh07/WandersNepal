Route overview

The most balanced first Nepal route links heritage, lowland wildlife and the Annapurna gateway.

This triangle works because each stop offers a distinct landscape: valley heritage, subtropical forest and a slower lakeside finish. Seven days is the practical floor; ten days gives each place breathing room.

Transportation

Tourist bus or private vehicle; flights on selected legs. Compare the full transfer—boarding point, luggage, stops and final arrival—not only the advertised travel time.

Last verified September 2026. Schedules and road conditions change; confirm directly before travel.

Major stops

  • Kathmandu — Begin with living heritage rather than using the valley only as an airport stop.
  • Chitwan — Choose ethical wildlife activities, keep distance and never accept guaranteed sightings.
  • Pokhara — Finish at a slower pace with lake, food and mountain-view options.

Where to stay & eat

Choose a Kathmandu base for heritage access, a Chitwan lodge that explains its wildlife practices, and a Pokhara neighbourhood that matches your preferred pace. In Chitwan, ask whether meals and park transfers are included before comparing rates.

Route alternatives

  • Reverse the route if onward travel from Pokhara is more convenient.
  • Replace Pokhara with Lumbini when pilgrimage matters more than mountain scenery.
  • Add Bandipur only with at least two spare nights; a rushed lunch stop misses its value.

Safety & useful tips

  • Choose wildlife operators that keep distance and do not offer elephant rides.
  • Avoid long road legs after dark, especially during monsoon disruption.
  • Keep one flexible half-day before flights or onward connections.
  • Carry water, medication and valuables in the cabin during transfers.

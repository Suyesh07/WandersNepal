Before you land

Practical advice from local trails.

Simple, transparent principles that make traveling across Nepal easier, safer, and far more rewarding.

Health & SafetyAcclimatize deliberately

Altitude illness does not respect fitness. Never gain more than 300–500m of sleeping elevation per day above 3,000 meters, and plan built-in rest days.

Cash & ConnectivityCarry physical currency

Beyond Pokhara and Kathmandu, mountain ATMs are often offline or empty. Carry sufficient Nepali rupees in cash for teahouses, showers, and charging fees.

Buffer TimeExpect mountain weather

Flights into Lukla, Jomsom, and Juphal are frequently delayed by cloud cover. Always build at least one buffer day before your international departure.

A shared responsibility

Travel thoughtfully. Leave less behind.

Filter drinking water to cut single-use plastic, support family-run homestays, compensate guides and porters fairly, ask before photographing people, and let sacred high-altitude paths remain clean.

Frequently asked questions

Planning your trip to Nepal.

Answers to the most common practical questions about safety, permits, trail seasons, and local customs.

SeasonsWhen is the best time to visit?

October to November offers the clearest mountain panoramas. March to May brings warm weather and blooming hills. For summer visits (June–August), rain-shadow regions like Mustang and Dolpo remain dry and ideal.

RegulationsDo I need a trekking guide?

Yes. In line with official safety and conservation mandates, international trekkers require a certified local guide for national park and restricted mountain trails across Nepal.

PreparationHow fit do I need to be?

Foothill walks and short treks suit anyone of moderate fitness. High-pass circuits (like Thorong La or Cho La) demand good stamina, steady pacing, and disciplined acclimatization schedules.

14 Days: The Classic Everest Base Camp Expedition

Two weeks is the international benchmark for visiting Nepal. For mountain travelers, it provides the minimum safe timeframe to ascend from sub-alpine river valleys to the lateral moraines of the Khumbu Glacier without compromising altitude safety.

This itinerary includes two mandatory acclimatization rest stops—at Namche Bazaar (3,440m) and Dingboche (4,410m)—following the golden physiological rule: climb high, sleep low. It finishes with an early morning summit ascent of Kala Patthar (5,545m) for panoramic alpenglow over Mount Everest, Nuptse, and Lhotse before a fast descent to Lukla.

Day 1: Flight to Lukla & Descent along the Dudh Koshi to Phakding

Day 01

Morning: Mountain Flight to Lukla (2,846m). Board an early morning STOL flight from Kathmandu (TIA) or Ramechhap/Manthali (RHP) to Tenzing-Hillary Airport. Meet your licensed Sherpa guide and porter team, organize duffels, and pass the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry checkpoint.

Trail: Lukla to Phakding (2,610m). Begin trekking down along the boulder-strewn Dudh Koshi river chasm. Cross suspension bridges festooned with prayer flags, passing hand-carved mani stones in Chhuthawa and Ghat.

Stats: 7.5 km · 3.5 hours walking · Net descent: 236 m. Overnight: Mountain teahouse in Phakding.

Day 2: National Park Gate, Hillary Bridge & Climb to Namche Bazaar

Day 02

Morning: Monjo & Sagarmatha National Park Counter. Follow the river through pine and blue-fir woods, crossing into Monjo (2,835m) to process your Sagarmatha National Park entry permit and pay conservation fees.

Midday to Afternoon: The Hillary Suspension Bridge & The Namche Wall. Cross the high Hillary Suspension Bridge spanning the confluence of the Bhote Koshi and Dudh Koshi. Settle into a rhythmic breathing cadence as you tackle the steep, zig-zagging 600-vertical-meter forest staircase to the horseshoe-shaped Sherpa capital of Namche Bazaar (3,440m).

Stats: 10.5 km · 5.5–6 hours walking · Net ascent: +830 m. Overnight: Stone teahouse in Namche Bazaar.

Day 3: Mandatory Acclimatization 1: Hotel Everest View & Khumjung

Day 03

Morning Climb High: Syangboche Ridge (3,880m). Ascend the ridge behind Namche to the old Syangboche airstrip, climbing up to the panoramic terrace of the Hotel Everest View for your first complete horizon view of Mount Everest (8,848m), Lhotse (8,516m), and Ama Dablam (6,812m).

Afternoon: Khumjung Village. Walk down to the green-roofed village of Khumjung (3,790m). Tour the historic Khumjung Monastery to view the preserved yeti scalp artifact and visit the Hillary School before descending to Namche for the night.

Altitude Strategy: Active acclimatization day. Sleep low at 3,440 m. Overnight: Namche Bazaar.

Day 4: The Panoramic Balcony Trail to Tengboche Monastery

Day 04

Morning: Contour Trail to Phunki Thenga. Walk the wide contour trail etched into the canyon wall above the Dudh Koshi with unobstructed perspectives of Ama Dablam. Descend past water-driven prayer wheels to the suspension bridge at Phunki Thenga (3,250m) for a hot lunch.

Afternoon: Forest Climb to Tengboche (3,860m). Ascend a 2-hour forested switchback through dwarf rhododendron and juniper to the ridge of Tengboche. Tour the spiritual heart of the Khumbu—Tengboche Gompa—and sit quietly inside the monastery courtyard to listen to the monks' afternoon puja ceremonies.

Stats: 9.2 km · 5 hours walking · Net ascent: +420 m. Overnight: Lodge in Tengboche or Deboche.

Day 5: Pangboche Heritage & Ascent to Alpine Dingboche

Day 05

Morning: Deboche Woods to Upper Pangboche. Cross the Imja Khola gorge on a steel bridge, walking past ancient birch trees to Pangboche (3,930m), home to the oldest gompa in the Khumbu. Pass the treeline where sub-alpine forest gives way to high-altitude scrub, dry pastures, and yak grazing paddocks.

Afternoon: Entering the Imja Valley. Follow the trail past the village of Somare, climbing gradually along the glaciated river basin into the stone-walled agricultural settlement of Dingboche (4,410m), sheltered beneath Ama Dablam and Lhotse Shar.

Stats: 10.8 km · 5.5 hours walking · Net ascent: +550 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Dingboche.

Day 6: Mandatory Acclimatization 2: Nangkartshang Peak Ridge Climb

Day 06

Morning High Hike: Nangkartshang Peak (5,080m). Take a slow, measured 3-hour uphill climb directly behind Dingboche. Gain valuable physiological adaptation above the critical 5,000-meter mark while taking in uninterrupted 360-degree vistas of Makalu (8,485m), Island Peak (6,189m), and the fluted ice face of Ama Dablam.

Afternoon: Recovery & Hydration. Descend back to Dingboche for lunch. Spend the afternoon resting in the heated communal dining hall, rehydrating with hot garlic soup, lemon-ginger tea, and water (target: 4 liters minimum).

Altitude Strategy: Active acclimatization day. Sleep low at 4,410 m. Overnight: Dingboche.

Day 7: Trek through Thokla Pass Memorial Ridge to Lobuche

Day 07

Morning: Dughla / Thokla (4,620m). Traverse the arid alpine plateau past Dughla, crossing the stream fed by the Khumbu Glacier terminal moraine.

Midday: The Climbers' Memorial Ridge. Climb the steep, rocky switchbacks up Thokla Pass (4,830m) to reach the emotional mountain memorials—chortens of stone and prayer flags dedicated to fallen climbers and Sherpas (including Scott Fischer and Babu Chiri Sherpa).

Afternoon: Lateral Moraine to Lobuche (4,910m). Follow the trail along the lateral moraine of the Khumbu Glacier to the cluster of lodges at Lobuche, set right beneath the jagged pyramid of Lobuche East.

Stats: 7.9 km · 4.5–5 hours walking · Net ascent: +500 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Lobuche.

Day 8: Gorak Shep & The Summit of Everest Base Camp (5,364m)

Day 08

Morning: Traverse to Gorak Shep (5,164m). Start by 6:30 AM across the undulating rocky debris and glacial moraine of the Khumbu Glacier, arriving at the final outpost of Gorak Shep in approximately 3 hours. Check into your lodge, eat a carb-dense lunch, and drink hot tea.

Afternoon: Push to Everest Base Camp (5,364m). Walk 2.5 hours along the ridge of the Khumbu lateral moraine, descending onto the glacier surface itself to reach the iconic Base Camp boulder covered in prayer flags. Stand beneath the towering seracs of the Khumbu Icefall, watching climbing expeditions and hearing the rumble of distant avalanches off Nuptse.

Stats: 12.5 km · 7–8 hours roundtrip · High point: 5,364 m. Overnight: Gorak Shep (5,164 m).

Day 9: Dawn Ascent of Kala Patthar (5,545m) & Descent to Pheriche

Day 09

Pre-Dawn Summit: Kala Patthar (5,545m). Begin your climb at 4:30 AM using headlamps in sub-zero cold (-10°C to -15°C). Step onto the prayer-flag-strewn rocky summit of Kala Patthar just as the first rays of sunrise strike the dark triangular pyramid of Mount Everest, Nuptse, and the Changtse wall across Tibet.

Late Morning & Afternoon: Rapid Descent to Pheriche (4,240m). Descend back to Gorak Shep for a warm breakfast, pack your gear, and retrace your steps past Lobuche and down the Thokla ridge, turning right into the wide glacial valley of Pheriche. The air immediately feels richer in oxygen, bringing fast physical relief.

Stats: 14.5 km · 7 hours total · Net descent: -924 m. Overnight: Lodge in Pheriche.

Day 10: Down Valley Descent through Tengboche to Namche Bazaar

Day 10

Morning: Pheriche to Deboche and Tengboche. Follow the Imja Khola back down past Pangboche, climbing the short hill to Tengboche to admire the monastery in the morning light.

Afternoon: Phunki Thenga to Namche. Descend through rhododendron woods to the river bridge at Phunki Thenga, then make the gradual climb back along the mountain contour trail to Namche Bazaar. Enjoy hot showers, fresh bakeries, artisan espresso, and Wi-Fi.

Stats: 14.2 km · 6 hours walking · Net descent: -800 m. Overnight: Namche Bazaar (3,440 m).

Day 11: Final Trail Leg: Hillary Bridge, Phakding to Lukla Town

Day 11

Morning: Descending the Namche Staircase. Take a steep, knee-jarring descent through pine forests down to the confluence below the Hillary Suspension Bridge. Check out through the Sagarmatha National Park gate at Monjo.

Afternoon: Final Uphill Stretch to Lukla (2,846m). Continue down the valley past Phakding, then make the final gradual uphill climb into Lukla. Conclude your trek, settle tips with your porters and Sherpa team, and enjoy a celebratory farewell dinner.

Stats: 18 km · 6.5–7.5 hours walking · Overnight: Mountain lodge in Lukla.

Day 12: Morning Mountain Flight from Lukla to Kathmandu

Day 12

Early Morning: Tenzing-Hillary Airstrip Departure. Board your scheduled STOL flight back to Kathmandu (or Ramechhap Airport with private car connection). Transfer to your hotel in Kathmandu or Patan for hot baths, clean clothes, and massage recovery.

Overnight: Hotel in Kathmandu or Patan.

Day 13: Mandatory Aviation Buffer Day & Heritage Exploration

Day 13

Essential Safety Cushion: Lukla flights are frequently grounded due to morning cloud cover, high crosswinds, or monsoon rain. This day acts as an essential flight buffer to protect your international flight home.

Cultural Exploration: If flights operated on schedule, spend this day touring the medieval brick courtyards of Bhaktapur Durbar Square, shopping for cashmere and spices in Thamel, and enjoying a farewell Newari dinner.

Overnight: Kathmandu.

Day 14: Final Souvenirs & International Flight Departure

Day 14

Enjoy a relaxed breakfast. Complete final souvenir shopping for loose-leaf Ilam orthodox tea and handmade lokta paper journals before transferring to Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) 3 hours prior to your international flight departure.

High-Altitude Nutrition & Teahouse Fare

Your body burns 3,500 to 5,000 calories a day at high altitudes while managing decreased appetite. Prioritize clean, carbohydrate-dense meals:

  • Dal Bhat Tarkari (The Trekker's Engine): Unlimited refills of steamed rice, yellow or black lentil soup (daal), spiced potato curry, and wild greens. It is fresh, prepared daily, and free of questionable meat storage.
  • Sherpa Potato Stew (Syakpa / Riki Kur): Hearty vegetable stew with thick handmade flour noodles, or traditional Sherpa potato pancakes served with fresh herb butter.
  • Garlic Soup: High-altitude lodges recommend fresh garlic broth; locals have long relied on it to support circulation and respiration in thin air.
  • Avoid Meat Above Namche Bazaar: Due to local Buddhist spiritual animal-protection laws in the Khumbu, no animals are slaughtered in the upper valley. All meat is portered or flown in on uncooled flights and carried uphill for days without refrigeration. Eat vegetarian above Namche.
  • Hydration Rules: Drink 4 liters of clean fluids every day (hot lemon-ginger tea, black tea, boiled water, electrolytes). Avoid alcohol completely until you return to Lukla.

Alternative 14-Day Itineraries in Nepal

If you prefer to avoid mountain aviation, restricted permits, or extreme cold, consider these alternative 14-day routes:

Pass-Crossing Circuit The Classic Annapurna Circuit (14 Days)

Traverse from sub-tropical rice terraces in Besisahar up through Manang's Tibetan villages, over the glaciated Thorong La Pass (5,416m), descending directly into sacred Muktinath and Lower Mustang.

Trans-Himalayan Cultural Safari Upper Mustang Overland & Trek (14 Days)

Travel the rain-shadow high desert into the walled capital of Lo Manthang, exploring prehistoric sky caves in Chhoser, 8th-century Nyingma gompas, and wind-carved red clay canyons.

A non-strenuous cultural overland journey: Kathmandu (3 Days) → Bandipur (2 Days) → Pokhara (3 Days) → Tansen/Palpa (2 Days) → Chitwan National Park (3 Days) → Patan.

Altitude Safety & Lukla Flight Realities

  • The Rule of Two Buffer Days: Lukla's Tenzing-Hillary Airport sits on a high mountain shelf operating strictly under Visual Flight Rules (VFR). Even in peak autumn, afternoon cloud shifts can ground all flights for 24 to 48 hours. Never book an international flight home on the same day as your Lukla flight. Keep Day 13 and Day 14 as cushions in Kathmandu.
  • Ramechhap (Manthali) Rerouting: During peak spring (March–May) and autumn (October–November), the Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN) shifts tourist flights from congested Kathmandu Airport (TIA) to Manthali Airport in Ramechhap. This requires a 4-to-5-hour midnight overland car transfer from Kathmandu to catch morning flights.
  • Recognizing Altitude Sickness (AMS): Throbbing headache, loss of appetite, nausea, and insomnia. Never ascend with symptoms of AMS. If symptoms worsen after resting or taking acetazolamide (Diamox), descend immediately. Severe symptoms (persistent cough, pink sputum, loss of balance) indicate HAPE or HACE, requiring emergency helicopter evacuation.
  • Comprehensive Helicopter Evacuation Insurance: Ensure your travel insurance policy explicitly covers emergency helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 meters without hidden distance or altitude exclusion clauses. Keep a physical copy of your policy and emergency assistance phone number in your daypack.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I trek to Everest Base Camp independently without a guide?

Under national trekking regulations, foreign travelers in protected mountain zones must be accompanied by a licensed Nepali guide arranged through an accredited agency. This guarantees trail safety, emergency coordination, fair local employment, and seamless checkpost processing.

How cold does it get on an EBC trek?

During peak seasons (October–November and April–May), daytime temperatures are pleasant for walking (10°C to 15°C). However, above 4,000 meters at Dingboche, Lobuche, and Gorak Shep, night temperatures regularly plunge between -5°C and -15°C. Teahouse bedrooms are unheated; pack a 4-season sleeping bag (rated to -15°C or -20°C) and a quality down jacket.

Can you actually see Mount Everest from Base Camp?

From Everest Base Camp (5,364m) itself, Mount Everest's summit is obscured behind the massive black shoulder of Nuptse. The iconic, panoramic view of Everest's summit pyramid is viewed from Kala Patthar (5,545m), which is climbed on Day 9.

21 Days: The Complete Trans-Himalayan Circuit

A three-week timeframe represents the gold standard of Himalayan wilderness trekking. Unlike shorter routes where roads and airstrips truncate the approach, this 21-day expedition traces a continuous, foot-powered journey through changing ecological zones: beginning in the subtropical banana groves of the Budhi Gandaki river canyon, detouring into the sacred Tibetan Buddhist sanctuary of Tsum Valley, ascending through the Tibetan Bhotia villages of Nubri, and concluding with a glaciated traverse over Larkya La (5,106m) into the Annapurna basin.

Because the Tsum Valley branch requires multiple days between 2,200m and 3,700m, your body gains deep physiological acclimatization before you ever face the high-altitude pass at Larkya La.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu & Expedition Permit Finalization

Day 01

Land at Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA). Meet your registered trekking agency team and licensed mountain guide in Patan or Thamel. Complete final biometric and physical passport document checks required for processing the restricted area permits for Manaslu and Tsum Valley at the Department of Immigration. Gear checks for sub-zero sleeping bags and mountain boots.

Overnight: Hotel in Kathmandu or Patan (1,350 m).

Day 2: Overland 4WD Drive from Kathmandu to Machha Khola

Day 02

Depart Kathmandu at 6:30 AM in a private 4WD Scorpio or rugged bus. Drive west along the Prithvi Highway past Malekhu, turning north at Dhading Besi and climbing over rural hill ridges to Arughat and Soti Khola, following the rugged dirt road along the Budhi Gandaki riverbed to Machha Khola (900m).

Transit: 160 km · 8–9 hours driving. Overnight: Teahouse in Machha Khola (900 m).

Day 3: River Chasm Trek: Khorlabesi, Tatopani to Jagat Fort

Day 03

Cross the Machha Khola and walk the undulating canyon trail past the natural hot water spring at Tatopani (930m). Cross long suspension bridges over the thundering Budhi Gandaki river, ascending cliff-cut stone steps through Doban and Yaruphant to reach the clean stone-flagged village square of Jagat (1,340m), where your Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP) and Restricted Area Permit (RAP) are inspected at the police post.

Stats: 14 km · 6 hours walking · Net ascent: +440 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Jagat (1,340 m).

Day 4: Salleri, Sirdibas, Philim & Climb to Lokpa (Tsum Gateway)

Day 04

Climb over a rocky ridge to Salleri for views of Sringi Himal (7,161m). Descend to the Gurung village of Sirdibas, cross the suspension bridge to Philim (1,570m), and pass through terraced millet fields. Continue past Ekle Bhatti, where the trail diverges: leave the main Manaslu Circuit to branch northeast into the steep, uninhabited pine and rhododendron gorge of the Syar Khola, climbing to the gateway hamlet of Lokpa.

Stats: 15 km · 6.5 hours walking · Net ascent: +900 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Lokpa (2,240 m).

Day 5: Entering Sacred Tsum: Dense River Forest to Chumling

Day 05

Descend through silent, moss-draped forests of hemlock, fir, and blue pine to cross the Syar Khola on a wooden cantilever bridge. Tackle steep switchbacks up out of the canyon into the open lower valley of Tsum—the sacred Beyul Kyimolung ("Hidden Valley of Happiness") blessed by Guru Padmasambhava in the 8th century. Reach Chumling (Lower Tsum), home to traditional stone houses, Mani walls, and views of the Ganesh Himal.

Stats: 9 km · 4.5–5 hours walking · Net ascent: +146 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Chumling (2,386 m).

Day 6: Upper Tsum Ascent: Rainjam to Chhokangparo

Day 06

Cross the suspension bridge over the Shiar Khola, passing through the hamlets of Rainjam and Gho. Climb past stone-walled barley and buckwheat terraces into the twin settlement of Chhokangparo (Upper Tsum). Here, the valley widens into an alpine basin flanked by the cliffs of Baudha Peak and Ganesh Himal II.

Stats: 11 km · 5 hours walking · Net ascent: +624 m. Overnight: Lodge in Chhokangparo (3,010 m).

Day 7: Milarepa’s Meditation Cave, Burji & Trek to Nile / Chhule

Day 07

Trek north along the valley floor to Burji village. Climb a short side-track to visit Piren Phu (The Pigeon Cave)—one of the sacred meditation caves of the 11th-century Tibetan saint Milarepa, containing ancient stone footprints, statues, and historical murals. Continue past carved Mani walls through Phurbe and Pangdun to Nile (Chhule), the northernmost inhabited village in the valley.

Stats: 12 km · 5.5 hours walking · Net ascent: +351 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Nile (3,361 m).

Day 8: Pilgrimage to Mu Gompa & Frontier Tibetan Wilderness

Day 08

Make the steady morning climb past alpine yak pastures to Mu Gompa (3,700m), the highest and most remote monastery in the Tsum Valley, established in 1895. Visit the ancient prayer hall, home to gold-leaf Tibetan scriptures and historic thankas. Take an afternoon ridge walk toward the glaciated passes bordering Tibet before returning down to Nile for the evening.

Stats: 9 km roundtrip · 4 hours walking · High Point: 3,700 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Nile (3,361 m).

Day 9: Descent via Rachen Nunnery to Chumling

Day 09

Follow the eastern bank of the river down to visit Rachen Gompa (3,240m), an active Tibetan Buddhist nunnery established in 1905, noted for its courtyard murals and large prayer hall. Continue your descent past Chhokangparo, enjoying downhill walking through pine woods to Chumling.

Stats: 16 km · 6 hours walking · Net descent: -975 m. Overnight: Lodge in Chumling (2,386 m).

Day 10: Exit Tsum Valley & Rejoin the Manaslu Circuit to Deng

Day 10

Trek from Chumling down the steep forest trail back to Lokpa. Cross the Syar Khola suspension bridge to rejoin the main Budhi Gandaki river trail on the Manaslu Circuit. Walk through bamboo and rhododendron groves, climbing past Nyak Phedi and crossing the river multiple times on steel bridges to reach the village of Deng.

Stats: 14 km · 6 hours walking · Overnight: Teahouse in Deng (1,860 m).

Day 11: Entering the Nubri Valley: Bihi, Ghap to Namrung

Day 11

Enter the Tibetan-influenced highland region of Upper Nubri. The trail winds through fir and oak canopies, passing carved Mani walls in Bihi and Ghap. Cross the Budhi Gandaki on a wooden bridge where the water thunders through a narrow rock chasm, ascending stone stairs into Namrung (2,630m), featuring apple orchards, stone gateways, and police permit checkposts.

Stats: 15 km · 6–6.5 hours walking · Net ascent: +770 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Namrung (2,630 m).

Day 12: Lihi, Sho & First Views of Manaslu from Lho Village

Day 12

Ascend through terraced barley fields through Lihi (2,900m) and Sho (3,000m), where local Bhotia residents dress in traditional chubas. Climb onto the ridge of Lho (3,180m), crowned by Ribung Gompa. Step onto the monastery hill for unobstructed sunset views of Mount Manaslu’s double-pointed summit towering directly above the village.

Stats: 10.5 km · 4.5 hours walking · Net ascent: +550 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Lho (3,180 m).

Day 13: Shyala Alpine Ridge to Samagaun (Nubri Capital)

Day 13

Follow the pine-scented forest trail to the high clearing of Shyala (3,520m), surrounded by a 360-degree panorama of Himal Chuli, Peak 29, and Manaslu. Continue along the glacial outwash plain into Samagaun, the largest Bhotia settlement in the region, featuring flat-roofed stone houses, extensive Mani walls, and yak pastures.

Stats: 8.5 km · 3.5–4 hours walking · Net ascent: +350 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Samagaun (3,530 m).

Day 14: Acclimatization Excursion: Manaslu Base Camp or Birendra Tal

Day 14

Option A (Strenuous): Take a steep 4-to-5-hour acclimatization climb up through birch forest and moraine to Manaslu Base Camp (4,800m) to see the icefall and expedition tents beneath the main summit.
Option B (Moderate): Hike 1.5 hours north to the turquoise glacial lake of Birendra Tal (3,450m) below the Manaslu Glacier, followed by a visit to the 500-year-old Pungyen Gompa.

Altitude Strategy: Active acclimatization day. Sleep low at 3,530 m. Overnight: Samagaun.

Day 15: Highland Yak Pastures to Samdo (Tibetan Border Village)

Day 15

Descend to the Budhi Gandaki riverbed, passing the trail branching to Birendra Tal. Follow the gentle, wide valley through juniper and birch shrubs, climbing past long Mani walls to reach Samdo (3,875m). Samdo was established in the 1950s by Tibetan refugees from the nearby border village of Kyirong, preserving authentic customs, yak husbandry, and high-altitude trans-border trade.

Stats: 8.5 km · 3.5–4 hours walking · Net ascent: +345 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Samdo (3,875 m).

Day 16: Ascent to High Camp: Dharamsala (Larkya Phedi)

Day 16

Descend to cross the Budhi Gandaki for the final time. Climb past the ruins of the historic Larke Bazaar trading post, contouring around the lateral moraine of the Larkya Glacier. Arrive at Dharamsala (4,460m), the high staging camp beneath the pass. Eat an early carb-rich dinner and turn in by 7:30 PM to prepare for an alpine dawn start.

Stats: 7 km · 4 hours walking · Net ascent: +585 m. Overnight: Basic stone shelter or tented lodge in Dharamsala (4,460 m).

Day 17: Cross Larkya La Pass (5,106m) & Glacial Descent to Bimthang

Day 17

Pre-Dawn Summit Push: Begin climbing by 4:00 AM by headlamp over rocky glacial moraine. Pass frozen alpine tarns, making a long, gradual ascent to the prayer-flag-draped crest of Larkya La Pass (5,106m) at sunrise. Enjoy panoramic vistas spanning Himlung Himal (7,126m), Cheo Himal, Kang Guru, and Annapurna II.

Descent: Descend a steep, icy moraine ridge (microspikes recommended) down into the broad glacial meadow basin of Bimthang (3,720m), set directly beneath the ice face of Mount Manaslu.

Stats: 16 km · 8–10 hours total · High point: 5,106 m · Net descent: -1,386 m. Overnight: Mountain lodge in Bimthang (3,720 m).

Day 18: Bimthang to Tilije: Rhododendron Forests & Dudh Khola

Day 18

Descend through the Dudh Khola river valley, moving from moraine through pristine old-growth forests of silver fir, birch, and rhododendron. Stop for lunch in the forest clearing of Gho (2,560m), continuing down to the Gurung and Magar village of Tilije (2,300m), famous for apple orchards and stone-paved lanes.

Stats: 14 km · 5.5 hours walking · Net descent: -1,420 m. Overnight: Teahouse in Tilije (2,300 m).

Day 19: Final Footpath to Dharapani & 4WD Transfer to Besisahar

Day 19

Trek 1.5 hours down past the suspension bridge over the Marsyangdi River to Dharapani (1,860m), where the Manaslu Circuit joins the classic Annapurna Circuit trail. Check out through the ACAP and police checkpoints. Board a private 4WD Scorpio jeep for the rugged, scenic drive down the Marsyangdi River gorge to Besisahar (760m).

Trek: 6 km (1.5 hrs) · 4WD Drive: 3.5 hours. Overnight: Hotel in Besisahar (760 m).

Day 20: Highway Return to Kathmandu & Celebratory Dinner

Day 20

Drive east along the Prithvi Highway through Dumre and Mugling, following the Trishuli River back into the Kathmandu Valley (approx. 5.5–6.5 hours). Check into your hotel for long hot showers, laundry, and a celebratory dinner with your guide and support team.

Overnight: Hotel in Kathmandu or Patan (1,350 m).

Day 21: Final Souvenirs in Patan & International Flight Departure

Day 21

Enjoy a slow morning in the valley. Complete your final shopping for loose-leaf Ilam orthodox tea, hand-knotted wool rugs, or singing bowls before transferring to Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) 3 hours prior to your international flight departure.

Elevation Profile & Acclimatization Strategy

The brilliance of combining Tsum Valley with the Manaslu Circuit is its natural altitude staircase. Trekkers spend nearly a week sleeping between 2,200m and 3,361m in Tsum, giving the body time to produce red blood cells naturally before rejoining the circuit at 1,860m and ascending into Upper Nubri.

Notice the two major acclimatization staging plateaus: Mu Gompa in Tsum Valley (Day 8) and Samagaun in Nubri (Day 14). Both days use the proven mountain strategy of active daytime climbs followed by sleeping low, reducing Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) risks before tackling Larkya La on Day 17.

Foodways & Teahouse Sustenance

Trekking for three weeks burns immense energy. Teahouse cuisine changes as you transition through the valleys:

  • Lower River Canyon (Days 2–4): Traditional organic Gurung and Magar dal bhat featuring freshly plucked mustard greens (saag), river fish, local eggs, and fresh seasonal vegetables.
  • Sacred Tsum Valley (Days 5–9): Tsum is a designated non-violence sanctuary (Shyagya). No animals are slaughtered in the valley by spiritual law. Food is strictly vegetarian: buckwheat flatbreads (phapar ko roti), potato pancakes, noodle soup (thukpa), and warm salted yak-butter tea (su-chya).
  • Upper Nubri & High Moraine (Days 10–17): Roasted barley flour (tsampa), hearty Sherpa vegetable stew (syakpa), garlic broth, and unlimited dal bhat. Eat vegetarian above Namrung for food safety.
  • Hydration Protocols: Drink 4 to 5 liters of clean fluid daily. Avoid alcohol entirely from Lokpa until safely down in Besisahar. Bring water purification tablets (chlorine dioxide) or a UV purifier to eliminate single-use plastic bottles along the trail.

Alternative 21-Day Expeditions in Nepal

If you have three weeks on the ground and want to explore other remote frontiers:

Trans-Himalayan Lake Wilderness Upper Dolpo & Shey Phoksundo (21–22 Days)

Fly to Juphal; trek past deep turquoise Lake Phoksundo into the medieval Bonpo realm of Shey Gompa and the Crystal Mountain, crossing high glaciated desert passes untouched by commercial tourism.

Far-Eastern Alpine Wilderness Kanchenjunga Circuit (21–23 Days)

Trek from the cardamom hills of Taplejung through pristine rhododendron and oak cloud forests to the northern base camp at Pangpema (5,140m) and south base camp at Oktang beneath the third-highest peak on earth.

The Three High Passes of Everest Everest Three Passes Trek (20–21 Days)

The ultimate Khumbu traverse: conquer Kongma La (5,535m), Cho La (5,420m), and Renjo La (5,360m), linking Everest Base Camp with the sacred turquoise waters of the Gokyo Lakes.

Restricted Area Rules & Safety Protocols

  • Mandatory Group Size & Licensed Guide: Under Department of Immigration law, solo trekking in Manaslu and Tsum Valley is prohibited. A minimum of two foreign trekkers accompanied by a government-licensed Nepali guide is mandatory to issue Restricted Area Permits.
  • Non-Violence Sanctity in Tsum (Shyagya): Tsum Valley has observed an unbroken pact of non-violence for over a century. Do not carry raw meat into the valley, do not harm wildlife or insects, and respect local vegetarian guidelines.
  • Crossing Larkya La Safely: The pass crossing on Day 17 requires microspikes or crampons for icy hard-pack moraines. Leave Dharamsala by 4:00 AM to cross the summit before midday gale winds sweep over the ridge.
  • Comprehensive Helicopter Evacuation Insurance: Ensure your travel policy covers emergency high-altitude helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 meters without altitude caps. In the event of severe AMS, HAPE, or injury, your licensed guide coordinates directly with emergency flight dispatch in Pokhara or Kathmandu.

Frequently Asked Questions

How difficult is the Manaslu & Tsum Valley Trek compared to Everest Base Camp?

The Manaslu and Tsum Valley Trek is physically more demanding than standard EBC. It involves 21 continuous days of walking, longer daily distances (6 to 8 hours), narrow cliff-cut gorge footpaths with suspension bridges, and basic teahouse infrastructure. However, it experiences far fewer crowds and offers a much deeper cultural immersion.

Can I charge devices and use the internet along this route?

Teahouses in lower villages provide solar electricity and Wi-Fi for a modest fee (NPR 300–500). In Upper Tsum and above Samagaun, electricity is limited and dependent on solar panels; charging can be restricted during cloudy weather. Carry a high-capacity power bank (20,000 mAh minimum).

What is the best season for this 21-day expedition?

Autumn (October to mid-November) is optimal, offering clear blue skies, dry trails, and stable snow conditions on Larkya La. Spring (late March to May) is also excellent, with blooming rhododendron forests in the lower gorges and wild alpine flora. Winter brings deep snow that closes Larkya La, while summer monsoon rains cause heavy landslides along the lower gorge.

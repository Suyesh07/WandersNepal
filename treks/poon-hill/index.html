Trek overview

The Poon Hill Trek is widely celebrated as Nepal's quintessential introductory Himalayan journey, perfectly tailored for first-time visitors, families, and travelers looking for a rich cultural experience without committing to extreme high-altitude expeditions. Winding through timeless Gurung and Magar settlements, terraced agricultural hillsides, and dense, wildlife-rich rhododendron forests, this moderate route delivers a remarkably rewarding introduction to teahouse trekking in the Annapurna foothills.

The trail culminates at the panoramic viewpoint of Poon Hill (3,210 m), where early morning sun rays illuminate a sweeping horizon of massive Himalayan peaks, including Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South, Fishtail (Machhapuchhre), and Nilgiri. This guide presents a comprehensive planning baseline, not a fixed commercial package. Daily walking distances, trailhead transfers, and personal pacing adjust dynamically according to individual fitness levels, prevailing weather conditions, and physical adaptation.

Altitude cannot be scheduled by ambition. While Poon Hill sits at a moderate elevation, listen closely to your body, stay well-hydrated, and maintain a steady pace across stone stairways.

Day-by-day itinerary

Walking times are broad planning ranges. Distance varies with the exact lodge, trail diversion and acclimatisation day.

The loop is carefully mapped out to balance rewarding daily ascents with cultural immersion in traditional hill villages.

Pokhara to Ulleri5–6 hr · Scenic drive to trailhead followed by stone stair climbs
Ulleri to Ghorepani4–5 hr · Trekking through dense rhododendron and oak woodlands
Ghorepani to Poon Hill and Tadapani5–6 hr · Early pre-dawn summit for sunrise panorama, then ridge walking
Tadapani to Ghandruk4–5 hr · Descending through terraced fields to a historic Gurung culture village
Ghandruk to Pokhara3–4 hr · Final morning walk to roadhead and private vehicle transfer back to Lakeside

Permits & guide requirements

Permits

Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) entry permit and TIMS card registration are required for all independent and guided trekkers.

Guide requirements

Suitable and approachable for many first-time trekkers; however, checking current government regulations regarding licensed local guides is always recommended.

Life on the trail

Accommodation

Established routes use comfortable teahouses or community lodges run by local families; expect cozy communal dining spaces, twin-share rooms, and basic shared sanitation facilities.

Food

Freshly prepared dal bhat, warming vegetable soups, momos, noodles, potatoes, and Tibetan bread are readily available at every teahouse stop along the route.

Water

Use boiled, filtered, or properly treated water supplies. Avoid purchasing single-use plastic bottles to help preserve the pristine mountain environment.

Weather & altitude

The broad trekking window is October–May, offering crisp skies and vibrant spring rhododendron blooms. Mountain weather can still bring sudden afternoon cloud cover, wind and rain showers. The route reaches 3,210 m at Poon Hill; steady pacing ensures an enjoyable experience.

  • Do not continue upward with worsening headache, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue.
  • Keep your sunrise summit morning flexible for weather clarity.
  • Carry lightweight layers for sunny days and cool mountain mornings.

Packing list

Packing efficiently for the Poon Hill loop balances lightweight daytime hiking layers with warm fleece garments for chilly mountain evenings.

  • Broken-in waterproof trekking boots or supportive shoes
  • Comfortable thermal base layers and insulating fleece jackets
  • Lightweight waterproof rain shell and wind-resistant trousers
  • Sun hat, UV sunglasses, warm beanie, and light gloves
  • Refillable water bottle and reliable chemical or filter purification tablets
  • Compact head torch with fresh batteries and a portable power bank
  • Personal medication, blister care plasters, and basic first-aid items
  • Offline maps, physical permits, and emergency contact details
  • Sturdy trekking poles to ease strain on steep stone stair descents
  • Small daypack for carrying daily trail essentials and water

Safety & responsible trekking

Use licensed, insured local staff; keep porter loads fair and manageable; stay strictly on established stone pathways; pack out all non-biodegradable waste; and show deep respect for local village communities, temples, and natural water spouts.

Alternative itineraries

  • Extend the trek toward Ghandruk or Landruk to explore deeper Gurung heritage.
  • Choose shorter 3-day variations if your overall travel schedule in Nepal is tight.
  • Combine the loop into a broader Annapurna Sanctuary or Circuit itinerary if seeking greater depth.
  • Adjust daily walking distances freely without feeling pressured by strict timelines.

Frequently asked questions

How difficult is Poon Hill Trek?

Moderate; the combination of approx. 35–55 km, stair-heavy ascents, and altitude up to 3,210 m makes it physically engaging yet fully achievable for active beginners.

What is the best season?

October–May is the premier planning window. Spring brings blooming rhododendrons, while autumn delivers crystal-clear mountain visibility.

Do I need insurance?

Yes. Confirm that your travel insurance policy covers basic hiking elevation, medical expenses, and local evacuation procedures.

Trek overview: The quiet Annapurna alternative

Perched on a high ridge directly opposite Mount Dhaulagiri (8,167 m), the Khopra Danda Trek (also known as Khopra Ridge) is one of the Annapurna region’s best-kept secrets. While thousands of trekkers flock to Poon Hill, Khopra offers an equally spectacular—and far less crowded—alpine panorama.

What sets Khopra Danda apart is its social impact. The route was pioneered as a non-profit community trekking network, meaning profits from your stay at community lodges directly fund local schools and community health clinics in villages like Swanta and Nangi.

For adventurous trekkers, the ultimate highlight of the route is an optional day trip from Khopra Ridge to the sacred alpine waters of Khayar Lake (4,560 m), sitting dramatically beneath the sheer wall of Annapurna South.

High Altitude Trail Warning: The optional day walk to Khayar Lake reaches 4,560 m over rugged terrain with steep drop-offs. Ensure you are well-acclimatised at Khopra Ridge (3,660 m) before attempting the lake excursion, and start before dawn to avoid afternoon clouds.

Route profile & elevation chart

Pokhara (820m) Ghandruk (1,940m) Tadapani (2,630m) Bayeli Kharka (3,450m) Khopra Ridge (3,660m) Swantha (2,210m) Ghorepani (2,880m) Pokhara

The trail climbs sharply through oak and rhododendron forests up to alpine pastures along the ridge before dropping into terraced farmland at Swanta and loop-connecting with Ghorepani.

Detailed 8-day classic itinerary

This balanced 8-day circuit provides a progressive climb to high altitude with a dedicated buffer day for the Khayar Lake excursion.

Day 1: Pokhara to Ghandruk (1,940 m) via Kimche

Drive 2.5 hours from Pokhara to Kimche, then enjoy a gentle 1.5-hour uphill trek through stone steps to the traditional Gurung village of Ghandruk.

1.5 hr trek · 2.5 hr drive
Day 2: Ghandruk to Tadapani (2,630 m)

Climb steadily through dense moss-draped oak and rhododendron forests, listening for Himalayan langurs along the trail.

4–5 hr · 7 km
Day 3: Tadapani to Bayeli Kharka (3,450 m)

Leave the main tourist trail toward Poon Hill behind. Ascend off the beaten track through quiet forests to reach alpine pastures overlooking Dhaulagiri.

5–6 hr · 9 km
Day 4: Bayeli Kharka to Khopra Danda / Ridge (3,660 m)

Traverse high pasture ridges above the tree line. Arrive at Khopra Danda early in the afternoon for stunning views down into the Kali Gandaki Gorge.

4–5 hr · 6 km
Day 5: High-Altitude Day Hike to Khayar Lake (4,560 m) & Return

A long, demanding day walk climbing high above the ridge to a sacred alpine lake surrounded by shrine markers and glacier walls. Return to Khopra Lodge for the night.

8–9 hr · Day hike
Day 6: Khopra Danda to Swanta Village (2,210 m)

A steep, scenic descent down the ridge through rhododendron forests into the terraced agricultural village of Swanta.

5 hr · 9 km
Day 7: Swanta to Ghorepani (2,880 m)

Cross a suspension bridge over the river, pass through Chitre, and rejoin the main trail leading up to the bustling hub of Ghorepani.

4–5 hr · 8 km
Day 8: Morning Poon Hill Hike; Trek to Birethanti; Drive to Pokhara

Catch sunrise at Poon Hill (3,210 m), descend through Tikhedhunga to Nayapul/Birethanti, and take a private jeep or bus back to Pokhara.

6–7 hr · Trek + Drive

Permits & mandatory guide requirements

1. Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)

Required for all trekkers entering the region. Foreign fee: NPR 3,000 per person (~$23 USD). SAARC nationals pay NPR 1,000. Obtained at NTB counters in Kathmandu or Pokhara, or issued via your trekking agency.

2. Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) Card

Fee: NPR 2,000 per person. Issued electronically through registered trekking agencies to track trekker safety.

Mandatory Guide Rule

Under current official provisions, foreign trekkers on National Park and Conservation Area trails are required to be accompanied by a licensed trekking guide and register through a recognized agency. This is especially valuable on the Khopra route, where high-altitude ridge weather changes rapidly.

Life on the trail: Community lodges & food

Community Lodges vs. Standard Teahouses

Between Tadapani and Swanta, accommodation consists mainly of community-run lodges. Facilities are clean and cozy but simpler than those in Ghorepani or Ghandruk. Expect shared bathrooms, cozy communal dining halls heated by wood stoves, and solar power for device charging.

Food & Local Produce

Meals are freshly cooked on-site. In addition to standard trekker staples like Dal Bhat, garlic soup, and apple pies, Swanta is famous for its locally produced cheese, fresh vegetables, and home-grown potatoes.

Water Management

Boiled water is available at all community lodges (NPR 150–300/liter). Bring reusable water bottles and water treatment tablets or UV purification systems to minimize plastic waste on fragile ridge trails.

Weather, season & high-altitude safety

Spring (March–May): The trail through Tadapani and Bayeli bursts with blooming red, pink, and white rhododendrons. Daytime weather is pleasant, though afternoon clouds can build over high ridges.

Autumn (October–November): Offers the clearest skies and most stable weather, presenting pristine, razor-sharp views of Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South.

  • Altitude Sickness (AMS): Do not ignore early symptoms (headache, nausea, dizziness). Always carry Diamox and descend immediately if symptoms worsen.
  • Weather Flexibility: Ridge trails are exposed to wind and sudden snow showers during late autumn or winter. Keep an extra weather contingency day in your schedule.

Essential packing list

  • Sturdy, waterproof trekking boots with good ankle support
  • Thermal base layers (merino wool recommended)
  • Mid-layer fleece and a high-loft down jacket (rated to -10°C for Khopra Ridge)
  • Windproof and waterproof shell jacket and pants
  • Warm wool beanie, sun hat, and UV400 sunglasses
  • Trekking poles (essential for the steep descent to Swanta)
  • Headlamp with extra batteries (crucial for early morning Khayar Lake hike)
  • Power bank (10,000–20,000 mAh) and universal plug adapter
  • First aid kit including blister treatment and rehydration salts

Safety & responsible trekking

By choosing the Khopra Danda route, you are directly supporting community education and rural health funds in the Myagdi district. Stay on designated paths to avoid soil erosion on fragile ridge crests, pack out non-biodegradable trash, and respect local cultural traditions around Khayar Lake by keeping the shoreline pristine.

Alternative itinerary options

  • Extend to Mohare Danda: Add another eco-community ridge trek for a longer 10–12 day off-the-beaten-path loop.
  • Skip Khayar Lake: If short on time or feeling high-altitude fatigue, skip the 4,560 m lake hike and rest at Khopra Ridge before descending to Swanta.
  • Connect with Annapurna Base Camp: Link Khopra Danda with the ABC trek from Tadapani for an extended 14-day Himalayan expedition.

Frequently asked questions

How difficult is the Khopra Danda Trek?

It is rated Moderate to Challenging. While regular walking days are moderate (4–6 hours), the day hike to Khayar Lake reaches 4,560 m and requires 8–9 hours of tough, high-altitude walking.

How does Khopra Danda compare to Poon Hill?

Khopra Ridge (3,660 m) is much higher than Poon Hill (3,210 m) and brings you significantly closer to Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South with a fraction of the trekker crowds.

Do I need travel insurance for this route?

Yes. Make sure your insurance policy explicitly covers emergency helicopter evacuation up to 5,000 meters altitude.

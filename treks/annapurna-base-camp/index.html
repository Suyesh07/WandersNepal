Trek overview

A steep-sided sanctuary route that climbs from village terraces into a close Himalayan amphitheatre.

This guide presents a conservative planning baseline, not a fixed commercial package. Daily distance, trailhead access and safe pacing change with the exact itinerary, weather and acclimatisation response.

Altitude cannot be scheduled by ambition. Add rest days and descend if symptoms worsen.

Life on the trail

Accommodation

Established routes use simple teahouses or community lodges; restricted and remote itineraries may need arranged camping. Expect shared dining, limited heating and variable charging.

Food

Dal bhat, soups, noodles, potatoes and porridge are common. At altitude, choose freshly cooked food and avoid over-ordering where supplies are carried in.

Water

Use boiled, filtered or properly treated water. Avoid a daily trail of disposable plastic bottles.

Weather & altitude

The broad trekking window is March–May · October–November. Mountain weather can still bring snow, cloud, wind and transport disruption. The route reaches 4,130 m; gradual ascent and honest symptom reporting matter more than a target day.

  • Do not continue upward with worsening headache, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue.
  • Keep the day before a high pass flexible.
  • Carry layers for sun, wind, cold and precipitation.

Safety & responsible trekking

Use licensed, insured staff; keep porter loads fair; stay on established trails; carry out non-biodegradable waste; and respect monasteries, fields and water sources. Your evacuation plan should work without a mobile signal.

Responsible trekking principles →

Alternative itineraries

  • Add an acclimatisation day rather than compressing the ascent.
  • Choose a shorter viewpoint route if time or experience is limited.
  • Use an overland approach when flight disruption risk is unacceptable.
  • Turn around or exit early without treating it as failure.

Annapurna Base Camp Trek Itinerary | 9 Days

A village-to-sanctuary route through Gurung communities, forested valleys and the high glacial amphitheatre of Annapurna Base Camp.

Day 1: Kathmandu to Ghandruk (2,012 m)

6–8 hr drive

Travel: Drive to Pokhara, then continue by jeep to Ghandruk.

Transition from the highway to rugged mountain tracks and arrive in a vibrant Gurung village with close views of Machapuchare and Annapurna South. Visit the Gurung Museum and explore the local culture.

Day 2: Ghandruk to Chhomrong (2,170 m)

6 hours

Descend steeply to cross the Kimrong Khola on a long suspension bridge, then climb to Chhomrong, a major gateway village with wide valley views.

Day 3: Chhomrong to Bamboo (2,310 m)

4–5 hours

Follow stone steps down to the Chhomrong Khola, climb past Sinuwa, and enter cool, shaded rhododendron and bamboo forests toward the river basin.

Day 4: Bamboo to Deurali (3,230 m)

3–4 hours

Move deeper into the sanctuary route through rugged cliffs, waterfalls and glacier-fed streams. Pass Dovan and Himalaya as the forest thins and the air turns alpine.

Day 5: Deurali to Annapurna Base Camp (4,130 m)

5–6 hours

Pass Machapuchare Base Camp at 3,700 m and enter the 360-degree glacial amphitheatre surrounded by Annapurna I and Hiunchuli. Move slowly in the thin, cold air.

Day 6: Annapurna Base Camp to Bamboo

6–7 hours

Watch the sunrise over Annapurna I before beginning the long descent. Although the route is downhill, trekking poles help reduce prolonged pressure on the knees.

Day 7: Bamboo to Jhinu Danda

5–6 hours

Continue down through the forest and hillside villages to Jhinu Danda, a natural place to slow the pace and recover after the high-altitude section.

Day 8: Jhinu Danda to Pokhara

Travel day

Complete the final trail section and transfer back to Pokhara for a warm shower, lakeside rest and an easy evening.

Day 9: Pokhara to Kathmandu

Travel day

Take a scenic highway drive back to the capital, leaving behind the rolling hills and snow peaks of the Annapurna region.

Annapurna Base Camp Altitude

The route climbs gradually from Ghandruk through Chhomrong, Bamboo and Deurali to Annapurna Base Camp at 4,130 m, then descends through Jhinu Danda to Pokhara. Elevations are approximate and can vary by lodge and trail conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Annapurna Base Camp Trek

What is the height of Annapurna Base Camp?

Annapurna Base Camp is situated at roughly 4,130 meters (13,550 feet) above sea level.

How long is the Annapurna Base Camp Trek distance?

The total distance of the route is around 70, 115 km, return, and it depends on things like the trailhead, parts where you need transport, the lodge's location, and the different route options.

How difficult is Annapurna Base Camp Trek?

TThe trek is moderate to challenging. Long walking days are a given, and you'll also deal with steep stone steps, changing weather, and a high point of 4,130 meters. Pace yourself, and don't rush the altitude gain.

How do I prepare for Annapurna Base Camp Trek?

Get fit for walking by training uphill and on stars, and also by carrying a light pack. Break in your shoes too. Be ready for cold, rain, and bad trails. Don't forget rest time and insurance.

What should I pack for Annapurna Base Camp Trek?

Pack these: trekking footwear that's broken in, warm clothes in layers, a waterproof shell, gloves, sun hat, refillable bottle with treatment, head torch, power bank, personal medication, first-aid stuff, and trekking poles.

What is the budget for the Annapurna Base Camp Trek , Cost?

Annapurna Base Camp (ABC) trek costs range from $500 to $1,300 USD per person for an independent trip. Fully organized guided tours range from $700 to $2,000 USD, depending on the trip length and group size.

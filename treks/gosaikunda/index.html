Trek overview

Located within the protected boundary of Langtang National Park, the Gosaikunda Trek is one of Nepal’s most accessible high-altitude pilgrimage journeys. Revered by both Hindus and Buddhists, the main freshwater lake resting at 4,380 meters is believed to have been created by Lord Shiva. Beyond its deep spiritual significance, the trail takes hikers through lush oak and rhododendron woodlands, rugged alpine ridges, and offers unobstructed vistas of Langtang Lirung, Ganesh Himal, and the Annapurna range.

Because the trail climbs steadily from Dhunche (1,960 m) to over 4,000 meters in just three days, proper pacing is essential. This guide presents a conservative planning baseline, not a fixed commercial package. Daily distance, trailhead access, and safe pacing change with your exact itinerary choice, weather conditions, and individual acclimatisation response.

Altitude cannot be scheduled by ambition. Add rest days, maintain constant hydration, and descend immediately if acute mountain sickness (AMS) symptoms worsen.

Day-by-day itinerary

Walking times are broad planning ranges. Distance varies with the exact teahouse selection, seasonal trail diversions, and individual acclimatisation pace.
Kathmandu to Dhunche (1,960 m)6–7 hr overland drive · Drive from Kathmandu through scenic mountain roads to the district headquarter of Rasuwa and prepare gear.
Dhunche to Chandanbari / Sing Gompa (3,330 m)5–6 hr trek · Continuous uphill climb through pine, oak, and rhododendron forests, stopping at the historic local cheese factory.
Chandanbari to Laurebina (3,910 m)4–5 hr trek · Steady ridge climb opening up dramatic, panoramic views of Ganesh Himal, Manaslu, and the Tibetan border peaks.
Laurebina to Gosaikunda Lakes (4,380 m)3–4 hr trek · Short, scenic high-ridge trail passing Saraswati Kunda and Bhairab Kunda before reaching the sacred Gosaikunda Lake.
Gosaikunda over Laurebina La Pass (4,610 m) to Ghopte (3,440 m)6–8 hr trek · Challenging climb up to the pass marked by prayer flags, followed by a long descent into the wild Helambu region.
Ghopte to Kutumsang (2,470 m)5–6 hr trek · Descend through fir and rhododendron forests across ridge trails and traditional Tamang and Sherpa settlements.
Kutumsang to Chisapani (2,215 m)5–6 hr trek · Enjoy continuous ridge walks with sweeping views of the central Himalayas before arriving at Chisapani.
Chisapani to Sundarijal & drive to Kathmandu3–4 hr trek + 1 hr drive · Final descent through Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park to Sundarijal and transfer back to Kathmandu.

Permits & guide requirements

Permits

Trekking to Gosaikunda requires two main documents: the Langtang National Park Entry Permit (approx. NPR 3,000 for foreign nationals) and the TIMS Card (Trekker's Information Management System). If you choose to extend your trek past Laurebina La and descend all the way into Sundarijal, an additional Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park Permit is required upon entering that zone.

Guide requirements

Always check current Nepal Tourism Board regulations prior to departure regarding mandatory guide policies in protected national park areas. Local certified guides are strongly advised as snow cover on the Laurebina La pass can obscure high-altitude trails rapidly.

Last verified: September 2026. Rules and permit fees can change based on seasonal government announcements; confirm details with the Nepal Tourism Board, protected-area authorities, or a registered local trekking agency before traveling.

Life on the trail

Accommodation

Established routes use simple, family-run teahouses or community lodges. Rooms typically feature twin beds with light blankets (bringing a zero-degree sleeping bag is recommended). Expect shared dining rooms centered around a firewood stove, limited solar heating, and variable battery charging stations charging nominal fees at higher elevations.

Food

Teahouse menus offer a mix of local and trekking classics: traditional Dal Bhat (lentils, rice, and vegetable curry), hot soups, garlic soup (great for altitude recovery), fried noodles, momos, potatoes, and morning porridge. At higher altitudes, choose freshly cooked vegetarian food to maintain health and avoid meat products carried up long distances.

Water

Always use safe water sources: order boiled water at lodges, or treat tap/stream water using reliable purification tablets or UV filters. Avoid buying single-use plastic bottles along the route to protect the fragile alpine ecosystem.

Weather & altitude

The primary trekking windows are Spring (March–May) and Autumn (October–November). During spring, rhododendrons bloom along the lower trails; autumn offers the crispest mountain visibility. High-altitude mountain weather can bring sudden snowfalls, freezing winds, and thick fog. Because the route reaches 4,610 meters at Laurebina La, gradual ascent and honest symptom reporting are vital to preventing altitude sickness.

  • Do not continue climbing higher if experiencing a worsening headache, persistent nausea, dizziness, or loss of balance.
  • Keep the day before crossing the high pass flexible in case of unexpected weather delays or necessary rest.
  • Carry versatile clothing layers suitable for variable conditions: warm sun, biting wind, extreme cold, and rain/snow.

Packing list

  • Broken-in, waterproof trekking footwear
  • Warm insulating down jacket and fleece layers
  • Windproof and waterproof outer shell jacket and trousers
  • Thermal base layers (merino wool or synthetic)
  • UV-protection sunglasses, sun hat, and fleece beanie
  • Refillable water bottle, insulated flask, and filtration tablets
  • Head torch with extra batteries and a high-capacity power bank
  • Personal medical supplies, altitude medication (Dimox), and blister care
  • Offline travel insurance papers and emergency evacuation contact details
  • Sturdy trekking poles with snow baskets

Safety & responsible trekking

Always trek with licensed, insured guides and support staff; ensure porter loads stay within fair weight limits (maximum 20kg). Stick to marked trails to prevent erosion, carry out all non-biodegradable trash, and respect sacred site etiquette around Gosaikunda Lake by avoiding improper bathing or leaving litter. Ensure your travel insurance policy covers high-altitude helicopter evacuation up to 5,000 meters and functions without cellular reception.

Alternative itineraries

  • Dhunche Out-and-Back (5 Days): Ascend to Gosaikunda and return directly along the same route to Dhunche, avoiding the Laurebina La pass crossing.
  • Langtang Valley + Gosaikunda Combination (11-14 Days): Start with the full Langtang Valley trek to Kyanjin Gompa before crossing over to the Gosaikunda trail at Thulo Syabru.
  • Gosaikunda to Helambu Circuit (8 Days): Cross Laurebina La pass and descend through Ghopte, Kutumsang, and Chisapani directly into the Kathmandu Valley at Sundarijal.
  • Turn around or alter your itinerary safely without treating changes in plans as a failure.

Frequently asked questions

How difficult is Gosaikunda Trek?

The trek is rated moderate to challenging. The primary challenge is the steep elevation gain from Dhunche (1,960 m) to Gosaikunda (4,380 m) over a short time frame, alongside variable trail terrain and cold mountain weather.

What is the best season?

March–May (spring wildflowers and clear mornings) and October–November (crystal-clear Himalayan views) offer the best stability. Always check current weather reports and snow levels prior to departure.

Do I need insurance?

Yes, comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory. Confirm that your insurance policy explicitly covers trekking up to 5,000 meters elevation, medical treatment, and emergency helicopter rescue services.

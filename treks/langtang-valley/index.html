Trek overview: Why choose the Langtang Valley?

Located just north of Kathmandu near the Tibetan border, the Langtang Valley Trek is one of Nepal’s most accessible and rewarding Himalayan journeys. Unlike the Everest or Annapurna regions, Langtang does not require expensive domestic flights; you can reach the trailhead at Syabrubesi via an overland bus or jeep drive.

Often referred to as the "Valley of Glaciers," Langtang takes you from subtropical oak and bamboo forests through stunning Tamang villages, up to high alpine pastures surrounded by towering 6,000m and 7,000m peaks like Langtang Lirung (7,227 m).

Following the tragic 2015 earthquake, the communities of Langtang have rebuilt with inspiring resilience. Today, trekking here directly supports local family-run teahouses, sustainable tourism, and cultural preservation.

Altitude Warning: While considered a moderate trek, you will sleep above 3,800m at Kyanjin Gompa. Never rush your ascent, take dedicated acclimatisation days, and descend immediately if severe AMS symptoms occur.

Detailed 7-day classic itinerary

This classic 7-day itinerary balances steady vertical gain with adequate acclimatisation time at Kyanjin Gompa.

Day 1: Drive from Kathmandu to Syabrubesi (1,550 m)

Overland drive via public bus or private 4WD jeep through Trishuli Bazar and Dhunche. (7–9 hours drive)

7–9 hr drive
Day 2: Trek Syabrubesi to Lama Hotel (2,480 m)

Cross the Bhote Koshi, climb through dense bamboo and oak forests along the roaring Langtang Khola. Watch for gray langurs.

5–6 hr · 11 km
Day 3: Trek Lama Hotel to Langtang Village (3,430 m)

Ascend steadily through Ghodatabela. As the valley widens, catch your first full view of Langtang Lirung before reaching the rebuilt village.

5–6 hr · 14 km
Day 4: Trek Langtang Village to Kyanjin Gompa (3,870 m)

A shorter, scenic walk past mani walls, chortens, and yak pastures. Arrive by lunchtime and visit the famous Kyanjin Cheese Factory.

3–4 hr · 7 km
Day 5: Excursion to Tserko Ri (4,984 m) or Kyanjin Ri (4,773 m)

Early morning summit hike for panoramic 360-degree views of Langtang Lirung, Yala Peak, and Dorje Lakpa. Return to Kyanjin for overnight stay.

6–8 hr · Day hike
Day 6: Trek Kyanjin Gompa back to Lama Hotel (2,480 m)

Retrace your steps downhill along the river valley. The descent is fast but demands attention to knee joints.

5–6 hr · 21 km
Day 7: Trek Lama Hotel to Syabrubesi; Drive to Kathmandu

Final short trek back to the trailhead, followed by private jeep or bus transport back to Kathmandu.

5–7 hr · Trek + Drive

Permits & guide regulations

1. Langtang National Park Entry Permit

Required for all foreign trekkers. Cost: NPR 3,000 per person (approx. $23 USD). Can be purchased at the Nepal Tourism Board office in Kathmandu or directly at the Dunche checkpost.

2. TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System)

Cost: NPR 2,000 per person. Issued through registered trekking agencies.

Are mandatory guides required in Langtang?

Under current official regulations, foreign trekkers are required to hire a licensed guide when trekking in Nepal's National Parks. Always verify the latest enforceability with local authorities or your trekking agency prior to starting out.

Accommodation, food & water

Teahouse Accommodation

Teahouses along the Langtang route are well-developed and comfortable. Rooms generally feature two single beds with mattresses and heavy blankets. Private attached bathrooms are available at lower elevations, switching to shared squat or sit toilets at higher altitudes.

Food & Dining

Teahouses offer surprisingly diverse menus, including traditional Dal Bhat (lentil soup, rice, and vegetable curry with free refills), Sherpa stew, fried noodles, momos, and hot porridge for breakfast.

Drinking Water

Boiled water can be purchased at teahouses (NPR 100–300 per liter). To reduce single-use plastic waste, carry re-usable bottles and use water purification tablets or UV purifiers.

Best season & weather conditions

Spring (March to May): Clear mornings, rising temperatures, and stunning hillsides covered in blooming red and pink rhododendrons.

Autumn (October to November): The golden window. Offers crystal-clear mountain views, stable weather, and pleasant daytime walking temperatures.

Winter (Dec–Feb): Cold with heavy snow potential at higher elevations; requires proper gear. Monsoon (Jun–Sep): High risk of landslides along the road drive to Syabrubesi and leech-infested forest trails.

Essential packing list

  • Sturdy, well-broken-in trekking boots
  • Moisture-wicking base layers and thermal tops
  • Fleece jacket and a warm down jacket (rated to -10°C)
  • Windproof and waterproof shell jacket
  • Quick-dry trekking trousers
  • UV-blocking sunglasses, sun hat, and warm beanie
  • Headlamp with spare batteries & power bank (10,000–20,000 mAh)
  • Personal medical kit including Diamox (for AMS), blister pads, and rehydration salts

Frequently asked questions

How hard is the Langtang Valley Trek?

It is rated Moderate. While there are steep uphill steps (especially on Day 2 & Day 5), the overall distance and altitude gain are manageable for anyone with basic physical fitness.

Can I complete this trek without flying?

Yes! One of Langtang's biggest advantages is that you do not need to fly on small mountain aircraft. Trailheads are reached by road drive from Kathmandu.

Is there internet or cell coverage on the trail?

NTC and Ncell SIM cards work in many parts of the valley up to Kyanjin, though connections can be spotty. Wi-Fi is available at most teahouses for a small fee (NPR 200–500).

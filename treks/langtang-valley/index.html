Langtang Trek

The Langtang Valley Trek is a moderate 7, 9 day journey in the Himalayas, beginning in Syabrubesi and reaching 4,800m at Kyanjin Ri. Close-up views of Langtang Lirung are a highlight, and trekkers also experience immersive Tamang culture; they can visit the Kyanjin Gompa yak cheese factory too.

Langtang Trek Itinerary | 8 days

Day 1: Kathmandu to Syabrubesi

Travel day

Primary focus: Travel logistics and settling in.

Solo tip: Head to Machhapokhari bus station in Kathmandu and catch a local bus or shared jeep. Sit near the front for views of the Trishuli River and a chance to talk with the driver. After arriving, check into a lodge and make sure your TIMS card and National Park permit are in order.

Day 2: Syabrubesi to Lama Hotel (2,470 m / 8,100 ft)

5–6 hours

Solo tip: Cross the Langtang Khola and climb steadily. Keep a moderate pace on the forested incline rather than rushing. Stop for tea and meet other independent trekkers at teahouses such as Bamboo.

Day 3: Lama Hotel to Langtang Village (3,430 m / 11,255 ft)

5–6 hours

Solo tip: Beyond Ghoda Tabela, the trail opens into a wider alpine valley. Visit the 2015 earthquake memorial. Langtang Village has been rebuilt, and its locally run lodges offer communal spaces where solo trekkers often meet in the evening.

Day 4: Langtang Village to Kyanjin Gompa (3,870 m / 12,696 ft)

3–4 hours

Solo tip: Use the shorter afternoon for acclimatisation. Visit the local cheese factory or Kyanjin Gompa, stay hydrated, and take time to enjoy the views of Langtang Lirung.

Day 5: Kyanjin Gompa to Tserko Ri and back (4,985 m / 16,354 ft)

7–9 hours

Solo tip: This is the trek's highest point. Arrange to hike with a partner, such as another trekker from your lodge the night before, and do not continue if weather deteriorates. An early start around 5:00–6:00 AM can improve your chance of clear views.

Day 6: Kyanjin Gompa to Lama Hotel (2,470 m / 8,100 ft)

6–7 hours

Solo tip: The descent is faster but can be tough on your knees. Trekking poles can help with balance; take breaks and stop for photos along the way, including at Thyangsyap.

Day 7: Lama Hotel to Syabrubesi (1,500 m / 4,921 ft)

5–6 hours

Solo tip: Finish the descent to Syabrubesi, where the air feels warmer and more humid. Celebrate the end of the trek with a good meal or write down your reflections.

Day 8: Syabrubesi to Kathmandu (1,400 m / 4,593 ft)

7–9 hours · Drive

Solo tip: Arrange return transport through your lodge the evening before, then take a morning bus or jeep to Kathmandu. Allow flexibility for road delays.

Langtang National Park

Trekking and mountaineering are popular in the park, which also offers opportunities to experience Tamang culture. Accommodation includes lodges, teahouses and camping.

  • Access and headquarters: Dhunche is about 117 km by road from Kathmandu and is home to the park headquarters.
  • Wildlife: Wild dogs, red pandas, pikas, muntjacs, musk deer, Himalayan black bears, Himalayan tahrs, ghorals, serows, rhesus monkeys, langur monkeys and snow leopards.
  • Birds: The park has 373 recorded bird species, including the tragopan and impeyan pheasant.
  • Vegetation: Habitats range from subtropical forests below 1,000 m to alpine shrubs and grasslands.
  • Best time to visit: October–November and March–May. Higher elevations remain cold; June–September is monsoon season, while December–February is snowy.
  • Attractions: Gosainkunda Lake, Langtang Valley and Helambu.
  • Entrance fees: NPR 25 for Nepali visitors, NPR 1,500 for SAARC nationals and NPR 3,000 for other foreign visitors.

Sources: Nepal Tourism Board — Langtang

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Langtang Trek?

Langtang Valley Trek takes around 7 to 8 days to complete, covering about 65 to 75 kilometers (40 to 47 miles) from Syabrubesi and back.

How much does the Langtang Trek cost for Nepali citizens?

For Nepali citizens, a 7-day Langtang Valley Trek typically costs NPR 15,000 to NPR 25,000 per person on a budget.

What should I pack for the Langtang Trek?

Pack light to reduce fatigue and move comfortably over steep terrain. A practical Langtang packing list includes:

  • Clothing: Moisture-wicking base layers, insulating and waterproof jackets, trekking pants, a warm hat, gloves, and a sun hat.
  • Gear: A supportive backpack, sleeping bag suited to expected temperatures, trekking poles, headlamp, UV-protective sunglasses, water bottles and purification, first-aid kit, dry sacks, and a trekking umbrella.
  • Footwear: Sturdy, supportive trekking boots and comfortable camp shoes or sandals.
  • Hygiene: Hand sanitizer, biodegradable soap, toilet paper, wet wipes, sunscreen, and lip balm.
  • Essentials: Nepali rupees, passport, permits, insurance documents, portable charger, snacks, and a lightweight daypack.

Trek overview

The Everest Three Passes Trek is the ultimate high-altitude teahouse circuit in the Khumbu region, designed for experienced mountaineers and ambitious trekkers seeking a complete traversal of Nepal's most legendary alpine corridors. Rather than simply visiting a single vantage point, this extensive expedition links three formidable high mountain passes—Kongma La (5,535 m), Cho La (5,420 m), and Renjo La (5,360 m)—while also incorporating stops at Everest Base Camp, Kala Patthar, and the turquoise glacial waters of the Gokyo Lakes.

This guide presents a comprehensive planning baseline, not a fixed commercial package. Daily distance, trailhead access, and safe pacing change with the exact itinerary, weather patterns, and individual acclimatisation response across extreme elevations.

Altitude cannot be scheduled by ambition. Add rest days, monitor oxygen saturation levels, and descend immediately if severe acute mountain sickness symptoms worsen.

Permits & guide requirements

Entering the Sagarmatha National Park and high-altitude restricted corridors requires proper documentation and registered administrative approvals.

Permits

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit and local Khumbu Pasang Lhamu rural municipality entry fee. Ensure all paperwork is processed in Kathmandu or at Monjo checkpoint.

Guide requirements

Experienced licensed support is strongly recommended due to technical pass crossings, route-finding across glaciers, and shifting weather; verify any mandatory government agency rules before departure.

Last verified: September 2026. Rules and fees can change; confirm with Nepal Tourism Board, the protected-area authority, and a registered trekking agency.

Life on the trail

Accommodation

Established routes use simple teahouses or community lodges; high pass sections feature basic stone shelters. Expect unheated rooms, shared dining halls, and variable battery-charging fees.

Food

Dal bhat, garlic soups, Tibetan bread, noodles, potatoes, and porridge are staples. At extreme altitude, choose freshly cooked meals and avoid over-ordering where provisions are portered or airlifted in.

Water

Use boiled, filtered, or chemically treated water. Avoid creating a daily trail of single-use disposable plastic bottles in fragile alpine valleys.

Weather & altitude

The broad trekking window is April–May and October–November. Mountain weather above 5,000 meters can change instantly, bringing heavy snow squalls, high winds, and severe sub-zero temperatures. The route reaches 5,535 m at Kongma La; gradual ascent and rigorous health monitoring are essential.

  • Do not continue upward with worsening headache, persistent nausea, extreme dizziness, or unusual fatigue.
  • Keep the days before crossing Kongma La, Cho La, and Renjo La flexible for weather windows.
  • Carry high-grade mountaineering layers, UV protection, and insulated gear for extreme cold.

Packing list

Equipping for the Three Passes requires technical alpine gear capable of handling freezing glacial winds, high UV radiation, and variable precipitation.

  • Broken-in waterproof mountaineering or trekking footwear
  • Heavy-duty down jacket and thermal insulating layers
  • Windproof/waterproof shell jacket and trousers
  • High-altitude glacier sunglasses (UV Category 4), sun hat, warm beanie, and fleece gloves
  • Insulated refillable water bottles and reliable purification system
  • High-output head torch with spare lithium batteries and power banks
  • Comprehensive personal medical kit containing altitude sickness treatments
  • Offline maps, insurance policy details, and emergency contact numbers
  • Sturdy adjustable trekking poles and microspikes for icy pass descents
  • Compact gear repair kit and heavy-duty blister care supplies

Safety & responsible trekking

Use licensed, insured staff; keep porter weight limits fair and humane; stay strictly on marked high-altitude trails; carry out all non-biodegradable waste; and respect local Sherpa culture, monasteries, and water sources. Your emergency evacuation plan must function without relying on cellular networks.

Responsible trekking principles →

Alternative itineraries

  • Add dedicated acclimatisation days rather than pushing across consecutive high passes.
  • Opt for the standard Everest Base Camp or Gokyo Lakes trek if technical pass conditions become impassable.
  • Utilise helicopter charter options when severe weather grounds flights out of Lukla.
  • Turn around or descend immediately without treating altitude adjustments as failure.

Frequently asked questions

How difficult is Everest Three Passes Trek?

Very challenging; the combination of approx. 160–180 km, extreme altitude reaching 5,535 m at Kongma La, technical ice sections on Cho La, and consecutive high-exertion days requires exceptional physical conditioning and prior alpine experience.

What is the best season?

April–May and October–November provide the most reliable weather windows for clear visibility and stable pass crossings. Always check local mountain forecasts and flight statuses before departure.

Do I need insurance?

Yes, absolutely. Confirm that your policy explicitly covers trekking up to 6,000 meters, high-altitude rescue, and emergency helicopter evacuation without financial caps.

Our Editorial Corrections Policy

At Wanders Nepal, accuracy is a safety imperative. In high-altitude environments like the Annapurna Circuit, Manaslu, or Dolpo, an outdated bridge status, an inaccurate water point, or an obsolete permit rule can jeopardize a trekker's health, timeline, or legal status.

We commit to immediate, transparent corrections:

  • Factual & Safety Updates: Reports concerning trail blockages, bridge washouts, landslide realignments, and medical outposts take precedence and are typically investigated within 12–24 hours.
  • Tariffs & Regulations: Municipal taxes (such as rural municipality entry fees), conservation fees (ACAP, MCAP, national parks), and Department of Immigration restricted area permit (RAP) rates are updated immediately upon official gazette confirmation.
  • Orthography & Cultural Nuance: We correct inaccurate romanization of indigenous names (Newar, Khas, Tibetan, Tamang, Kirat, and Magar terms) to ensure authentic cultural representation across all destination guides.
  • Transparent Edit Logs: Significant logistical alterations are recorded with timestamps at the head of every guide so travelers know when ground conditions were last confirmed.

Our Verification Workflow

Every submitted field report undergoes a systematic vetting process before publication to prevent false alarms and ensure safety consistency:

Step 01 Triangulation

We cross-check incoming reader reports against field observations from licensed guides operating with our partner network in the specific valley.

Step 02 Authority Verification

Regulatory claims (such as new checkposts or permit changes) are verified directly with the DNPWC, NTB, or local rural municipality offices.

Step 03 Publication & Attribution

Confirmed corrections are deployed live across the site with an updated "Last Verified" date. If requested, we happily credit contributors by name.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly are critical trail hazards posted?

If a report concerns a major route impassability (such as a destroyed suspension bridge or critical pass closure), we verify with local police or guide unions and post prominent warning notices within 24 hours.

Can I submit photos or GPS tracks with my report?

Yes. If you have GPX files, photos of washed-out bridges, or receipts for revised municipal tariffs, note them in your submission or email them directly to editor@wandersnepal.com.

Do you pay for route corrections?

We operate an independent, community-driven resource. While we do not offer financial bounties for updates, we credit our field contributors and offer priority route-planning consultations for active contributors.
